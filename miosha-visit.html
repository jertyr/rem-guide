The Site Visit

  • No notice
  • The compliance officer asked us to put our tools down and started the inspection right away
  • He stayed an hour or two
  • Made notes, interviewed the carpenters, took photos
  • Asked for documentation

On site

  • Lead carpenter, intermediate carpenter, junior carpenter

Work going on

  • Cutting fiber cement with an angle grinder and HEPA vac
  • Drywall patch, done outside
  • Miter saw set up

What Do You See?

What would an inspector see in each of these?

Penalty

  • Proposed penalty: $900
  • Reduced to $450 by signing the Penalty Reduction Agreement (50% off)
  • Repeat citations (same violation within 3 years of the case closing) are 2x the first time and 5x the second for companies with 250 or fewer employees
  • MIOSHA can go up to 10x when they think it's needed

Source: MIOSHA Field Operations Manual, repeat violations

Deadlines

  • 15 working days from receiving the citations to sign the Penalty Reduction Agreement or file an appeal (MIOSHA prefers within 5)
  • For construction, everything has to be corrected before they approve the agreement
  • 15 working days after approval to pay, or the full penalty is due
  • Post the approved agreement for 3 working days, or until everything is corrected
  • Signing the agreement means you accept the citations and can't contest them

Our dates

  • June 23: citations issued
  • July 1 and 2: training and updated plan
  • July 6: agreement and proof sent
  • July 13: approved
  • July 22: paid (due August 3)

Source: MIOSHA Agency Instruction COM-04 and our signed agreement

Updated Safety Plan

We went from a 3-ring binder to an electronic document everyone can access.

Old plan (2016)

  • Old address and committee members
  • Mostly sample language
  • No silica plan or respirator program
  • Still said MSDS

New plan (2026)

  • Cites the OSHA / MIOSHA standard in each section
  • Competent person named on every job
  • Silica exposure control plan
  • Respirator and fit test section
  • Training notes for toolbox talks
  • Forms: JSA, site inspection, incident report, training sign-off, emergency posting

Training

  • June 17 and July 1: First Aid / CPR classes
  • July 2: ladder safety and silica awareness (sign-in sheet)
  • July 16: makeup session
  • November 6: EPA RRP lead refresher due
  • Training dates tracked by employee
  • Short training we run ourselves: ladders, silica, fall protection, hazard communication, PPE (crew safety training)

Vehicle Safety Kits

In every work vehicle

  • Safety glasses
  • Hard hat
  • Hearing protection
  • N95 dust masks
  • P100 respirator
  • Work gloves
  • Safety boots
  • Hi-vis vest
  • Voltage pen tester
  • First aid kit
  • Fire extinguisher (2A)
  • GFCI cord

How we track it

  • Checklist by vehicle number
  • We buy whatever is missing
  • Red safety bag left on bigger jobs

Safety Resources Folder in BuilderTrend

Started the day after the visit. Everyone can open it on their phone.

  • First Aid / CPR and lead certifications
  • Safety Data Sheets for the products we use (22 so far)
  • Emergency signs: nearest hospital, trauma center, work injury clinic
  • Current safety plan
  • Training and PPE tracker by employee
  • EPA RRP lead forms

Takeaways

  1. Have proof of certifications on site
  2. Name a competent person on every job
  3. Get out your written plan and look it over. Ours was 10 years old.
  4. Do toolbox talks and document them as a daily log
  5. Put together red safety bags
  6. As a contractor you are responsible for the safety of everyone else on your job site
