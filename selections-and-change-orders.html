Selections and Change Orders

Late selections and unwritten changes are where most remodel schedules and budgets slip. Both are manageable with due dates set from the schedule and a signed piece of paper before any money or time changes hands.

Quick Reference

  • Every selection has a due date set by its lead time and when it is needed on site, not by the start date
  • Nothing gets ordered until the client signs off on the exact item
  • Track every selection: picked, approved, ordered, confirmed, received
  • Allowances state the dollar amount and whether install is included
  • No extra work starts without a signed change order
  • Every change order states the price and the schedule impact
  • Settle overages and credits as they happen, not on the final invoice

Selections

  • Build the selections schedule at contract
    • List every item the client still has to choose
    • Find the date each item is needed on site from the job schedule
    • Back up from that date by the supplier's current lead time plus a buffer. That is the due date
    • Get current lead times from the supplier at contract. They change with the season and the market
  • Common long-lead items
    • Windows and exterior doors
    • Cabinets
    • Special order tile and stone
    • Plumbing fixtures, especially non-stock finishes
    • Appliances
    • Interior doors and stain grade trim
    • Light fixtures
  • Specify completely
    • Brand, model or SKU, size, finish or color, and quantity
    • Everything the item needs to work: valve and trim, sink and faucet hole count, appliance and its install kit
    • Use the order forms for windows and doors so nothing gets missed. See Resources
  • Order and confirm
    • Client signs off on the exact item before the order goes in
    • Check the order acknowledgement against the selection line by line
    • Record the confirmed ship date and follow up before it
    • Check every delivery against the order when it arrives, not when it is installed

Allowances

  • An allowance is a budget for an item the client has not picked yet
  • Write each one as a dollar amount, and say whether it is material only or includes install
  • Set allowances from what clients in that price range actually pick, not the cheapest option on the shelf
  • When the selection is made, show the client the difference: over is a charge, under is a credit
  • Put the overage or credit on a change order when the selection is made

Change Orders

  • Where they come from
    • Client requests: added scope, upgrades, or removed items
    • Hidden conditions found on site, such as rot, bad wiring, or an undersized beam
    • Inspector or code-required work not in the original scope
    • Allowance overages and credits
  • What every change order includes
    • A plain scope description: what is added, removed, or changed
    • The price, or the credit
    • The schedule impact in days, even when it is zero
    • When payment is due
    • Client signature and date
  • Process
    • Field crew does not take on extra work from a verbal ask. They tell the client "I'll get that to the PM" and pass it on the same day
    • PM writes, prices, and sends the change order
    • Client signs before the work starts
    • For hidden conditions that stop the job, photograph them, call the client, and get the signature before the wall closes
    • Log no-charge changes too. They still change the scope and the record

Best Practices

  • Hand the client the selections schedule at the contract signing, with due dates on it
  • Hold a selections meeting before demo, not after
  • Keep one list of selections per job, in one place everyone can see
  • Write the change order the day the change comes up. Memory gets worse and the price gets harder to defend
  • Small changes still get paper. Ten small changes add up to a big one

Client Communication

  • Explain at contract that a late selection moves the schedule, and by how much
  • Explain that allowances are budgets, not prices, and how overages are billed
  • Set the rule up front: changes go through the PM and are signed before the work happens
  • Give the client a running total of approved change orders so the final invoice holds no surprises
