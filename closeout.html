Closeout and Punch List

Closeout is everything between "the work is done" and "the job is done": the punch list, final inspections, cleaning, paperwork, and the final payment. It sets the client's lasting impression of the whole project, and it drags on for weeks when nobody owns it.

Quick Reference

  • One punch list per job, one owner, kept in one place
  • Walk the job yourself before the client walks it
  • Schedule final inspections when the last trade finishes, not when the punch list is done
  • Every punch item gets a location, a responsible party, and a date
  • Settle allowance overages and open change orders before the final invoice, not on it
  • Bring the painter back once, at the end, for all touch-up
  • Hand over warranties, manuals, paint, and attic stock as one package
  • Define "done" at the start of the job: finals passed, punch list signed off, closeout package delivered, final invoice paid

Prerequisites

  • All trades finished, including plumbing, electrical, and HVAC trim out
  • Final paint coats on, hardware and accessories installed
  • Appliances set and running
  • Every change order signed
  • Rough clean done so the walk is about the work, not the dust

Process

  • Internal walk (pre-punch)
    • Lead carpenter and PM walk the job room by room before the client sees it
    • Operate everything: doors, windows, drawers, faucets, toilets, lights, fans, appliances
    • Test every receptacle with a tester; test GFCI and AFCI devices
    • Run water and check under every sink and behind every toilet for leaks
    • Run the HVAC and check every register for airflow
    • Fix what you can on the spot. Order replacement parts for damaged items now, not after the client finds them
  • Final inspections
    • Schedule trade finals (electrical, plumbing, mechanical) as soon as each trade is done
    • Building final after the trade finals pass
    • Additions and newly finished spaces may need a certificate of occupancy or final approval before they can be legally occupied. Confirm with the building department at permit, not at the end
    • Keep the signed permit card or final approval for the closeout package
  • Client walkthrough
    • Schedule it in daylight and allow an hour or more for a full kitchen or addition
    • Walk room by room with the client and mark items with low-tack painter's tape
    • Write each item with room, location, description, who owns it, and target date
    • Agree that this is the list. Items found later go to the warranty process; new requests go on a change order
    • Client initials the list. Give them a copy the same day
  • Work the list
    • Group items by trade and get each trade back in one trip
    • Painter goes last, after every other trade has touched the walls
    • Update the list as items close and photograph completed items
    • Re-walk with the client and get the list signed off
  • Final clean and demob
    • Pull floor and surface protection; clean the floor it was protecting
    • Clean inside cabinets and drawers, windows and tracks, fixtures, and registers
    • Remove stickers and labels from fixtures, glass, and appliances
    • Replace the HVAC filter. Construction dust loads it fast
    • Haul out leftover material; schedule dumpster, portable toilet, sign, and lockbox pickup
  • Closeout package
    • Builder warranty terms and how to report a warranty item
    • Manufacturer warranties and manuals for windows, doors, fixtures, appliances, and equipment
    • Selections record: paint brand, color, and sheen by room; tile and grout; flooring; fixtures
    • Leftover paint labeled by room, and attic stock of tile and flooring
    • Photos of walls and ceilings before drywall, showing framing, plumbing, wiring, and blocking
    • Permit final or certificate of occupancy
    • Final lien waivers from subs and suppliers where the contract or a lender calls for them
  • Financial closeout
    • Reconcile every allowance and show the credit or charge
    • Confirm all change orders are signed and billed
    • Walk the client through the final invoice before it goes out so nothing on it is a surprise
  • Internal closeout
    • Compare estimated and actual cost and labor hours by cost code
    • Note what went wrong and what to change on the next estimate or schedule
    • Put a warranty check-in on the calendar before the warranty period ends

Best Practices

  • Have each trade punch its own work before leaving each phase. A short final punch list is built during the job, not at the end
  • Photograph every wall and ceiling before drywall. It is the most useful thing in the closeout package years later
  • Keep the punch list to the agreed scope. A list that keeps growing stops getting finished
  • Set a target to close the list, such as two weeks from the client walk, and say it out loud
  • Demonstrate GFCI reset, fan controls, filters, and anything else the client will need to operate, instead of telling them it works
  • Leave a short list of first-year items: seasonal gaps in wood, nail pops during the first heating season, caulk joints, and grout sealing

Inspections

  • Finals usually confirm the permit scope is complete and the trades signed off
  • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms installed and working where required (IRC R314, R315). Remodel permits often trigger alarm upgrades in the rest of the house
  • Handrails and guards complete. See Stairs
  • GFCI and AFCI protection in place. See Electrical
  • Use the Trade Inspection Checklist to prep the trades

Client Communication

  • Explain the punch process at the start of the job: one walk, one list, one sign-off
  • Explain the final invoice before it arrives: allowance reconciliation and change orders
  • Small seasonal cracks and nail pops in the first year are normal and get handled at the warranty check-in
  • Tell the client what the warranty covers and exactly how to report an item
  • Ask for a review or referral after sign-off, not before
