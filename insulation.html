Insulation

Insulation goes in after the rough building inspection and before drywall, sometimes with the exception of loose fill attic insulation that can be installed after the drywall lid is up. This page covers materials and methods for insulation with a focus on Michigan requirements for climate zones 5 and 6 and fire code clearances, but this is broadly applicable across the US and in other areas with codes based on the IRC.

Quick Reference

  • Rough building inspection must pass before wall/ceiling insulation begins
  • Wall and ceiling insulation must be inspected before drywall goes up
  • Attic insulation can be installed after drywall as long as it's accessible for inspection. Loose fill installers use depth markers (measuring sticks) to make verification easy
  • Spray foam requires an insulation certificate because inspector can't verify R-value in the field
  • Combustible insulation must be 3" from recessed lights, fan motors, and heat-producing devices (R302.13)
  • Kraft facing must be torn away 3" near light fixtures and fans (R302.13)
  • Foam board cannot be left exposed in basements or crawl spaces unless it meets ASTM E84 flame spread/smoke requirements
  • Below-slab insulation: use EPS, not XPS or polyiso (absorb water and lose R-value)

Code Requirements (2015 Michigan Residential Code)

Michigan spans two IECC climate zones: Zone 5 covers southern Michigan (roughly south of I-96/Lansing), and Zone 6 covers northern Michigan and the Upper Peninsula. See the DOE Building America climate zone map for detailed boundaries.

Assembly Zone 5 Zone 6
Wood frame walls R-20 (or R-13 + ci) R-20 (or R-13 + ci)
Ceilings R-38 R-49
Basement walls R-13 (or R-10 ci) R-19 (or R-15 ci)
Crawl space walls R-19 (or R-15 ci) R-19 (or R-15 ci)
Windows / doors / skylights U-0.32 U-0.32

Source: 2015 MRC Chapter 11 (Energy Efficiency), based on 2015 IECC Table R402.1.2
ci = continuous insulation (exterior sheathing or foam board without thermal bridging through framing)

Materials

  • Fiberglass Batts
    • 2×4 walls: R-13
    • 2×6 walls: R-19 (note: R-19 alone does not meet the R-20 code minimum)
    • Ceiling: R-38 faced batts available in 12.5" thick; can be friction-fit without staples (per Owens Corning)
    • Kraft facing must be torn away 3" from recessed lights, fans, and heat-producing devices (R302.13)
  • Mineral Wool (Rockwool Comfortbatt)
    • 2×4 walls: Comfortbatt 15 (R-15)
    • 2×6 walls: Comfortbatt 23 (R-23)
    • Naturally fire-resistant, water-repellent, and sound-dampening; fits friction-tight in standard cavities
  • Cellulose
    • Attic loose fill: R-3.6 per inch; R-38 at 11" thick, R-49 at 14" thick
    • Wall dense-pack: R-3.8 per inch, dense-packed to 3.5 lb/ft³ or higher to prevent settling
    • Example cavity totals: 2×4 wall R-13.3, 2×6 wall R-20.9
  • Spray Foam
    • Open-cell: ~0.5 lb/ft³ density, ~R-3.6 per inch
    • Closed-cell: ~2 lb/ft³ density, ~R-6.5 per inch
    • Spray foam requires an insulation certificate for inspection because inspector cannot verify R-value visually
    • Closed-cell also serves as an air barrier and vapor retarder at ≥2" thickness
  • Foam Board (Rigid)
    • EPS (expanded polystyrene): ~R-3.9 per inch, waterproof. Use this below slabs (does not absorb water like XPS/polyiso)
    • XPS (extruded polystyrene): ~R-5 per inch, 25 psi compressive strength; Kingspan GreenGuard claims moisture resistant but not waterproof
    • Polyiso (polyisocyanurate): ~R-6 per inch, not waterproof
    • Exposure limits: EPS, XPS, and polyiso cannot be left exposed in basements or crawl spaces because they do not meet ASTM E84 flame spread/smoke development requirements
    • Exception: Foil-faced polyiso (e.g., Johns Manville AP Foil-Faced, available at Lowes) is ASTM E84 compliant. Do not confuse with EPS foam with mylar facing, which is not compliant

Process

  • Pre-Insulation Check
    • Confirm rough building inspection has passed
    • Verify fireblocking and draftstopping are complete
    • Confirm all walls and ceilings are clean of nails or screws and blocked for drywall
    • Air-seal all penetrations before insulating: top plates, bottom plates, wiring and plumbing holes, duct boots, and any gap between framing and sheathing
    • Install ventilation baffles (rafter vents) at every rafter bay along the eaves before blowing or placing attic insulation to maintain soffit-to-ridge airflow
  • Batt Installation (Fiberglass / Mineral Wool)
    • Cut batts to fit snug with no compression, no gaps, no voids around wiring or plumbing
    • Split batts around wires: cut the batt in half through its thickness so insulation is on both sides of the wire, not compressed behind it
    • Cut and piece around electrical boxes instead of stuffing insulation behind the box
    • Kraft-faced batts: face toward conditioned space; tear facing away 3" from recessed lights, fans, and heat-producing devices (R302.13)
    • R-38 ceiling batts can be friction-fit without staples (per Owens Corning)
  • Dense-Pack Cellulose (Walls)
    • Install netting or membrane across stud faces to contain the fill
    • Dense-pack to 3.5 lb/ft³ or higher to prevent settling
    • Fill from bottom up through netting with insertion tube; listen and feel for consistent density
  • Loose-Fill Cellulose (Attics)
    • Blow to depth markers by setting attic rulers at required depth (11" for R-38, 14" for R-49)
    • Keep insulation away from recessed lights and flues per fire clearance requirements
    • Do not block soffit vents; ventilation baffles must be in place before blowing begins
  • Spray Foam
    • Requires a licensed applicator; other trades should be off the floor during application
    • Applicator provides insulation certificate with R-value, thickness, and product data for the inspector
    • Excess foam that extends past stud faces must be trimmed flush before drywall
  • Foam Board / Continuous Insulation
    • Below-slab: use EPS because it does not absorb water; XPS and polyiso absorb water and lose insulating value
    • Basement / crawl space walls: foam board must be covered or use ASTM E84-compliant product (e.g., Johns Manville AP Foil-Faced polyiso)
    • Seal all seams and edges with foam-compatible tape or canned spray foam to create a continuous air and thermal barrier
  • Foundation and Crawl Space Sequencing
    • Crawl space walls should be insulated before framing begins above
    • In some cases, insulation is inspected before pouring a concrete slab in a crawl or basement

Attic Ventilation (R806)

  • Standard Rule (1:150)
    • Enclosed attics and enclosed rafter spaces must be ventilated unless intentionally built as unvented conditioned assemblies (R806)
    • Minimum net free area (NFA): 1 sq ft of vent opening per 150 sq ft of attic floor area
    • Example: 1,500 sq ft attic requires 10 sq ft total NFA, split between intake (soffit) and exhaust (ridge or roof vents)
  • Reduced Ratio Exception (1:300)
    • Ventilation area can be cut to 1 sq ft per 300 sq ft when either condition is met:
      • A Class I or II vapor retarder is installed on the warm-in-winter side of the ceiling, OR
      • At least 40% but not more than 50% of the vent area is located within ~3 feet of the ridge, with the balance at the eaves
    • Many inspectors accept painted drywall ceiling as a Class II vapor retarder for this exception. A balanced ridge-and-soffit system designed at 1:300 typically passes
  • Installation Requirements
    • Vents must be pest-screened and open directly to outside
    • Keep at least 1" clear between insulation and roof sheathing at the eaves
    • Install baffles (rafter chutes) in every rafter bay at the soffit; run them up past the insulation line to keep the air channel open to the ridge

Fire Code and Insulation

  • Keep all combustible insulation and kraft facing 3" away from recessed lights, fan motors, and other heat-producing devices; tear kraft facing back at each fixture (R302.13)
  • Insulation used as fireblocking: batts/blankets of mineral wool or glass fiber must be securely retained and fill the entire cavity tightly 16" around the opening between wall and floor or ceiling and packed tight around pipes
  • Loose-fill insulation does not qualify as fireblocking
  • Foam board left exposed in basements or crawl spaces does not meet ASTM E84 flame spread/smoke development requirements and must be covered with ignition barrier or use a compliant product

Inspections

  • Insulation Inspection: Required after rough building inspection passes and before walls/ceilings are covered (before drywall)
  • Inspector verifies all spaces are insulated to code minimums for the climate zone
  • Spray foam installations require an insulation certificate from the applicator because inspector cannot verify R-value in the field
  • Inspector checks for gaps, voids, compression, and proper contact with air barrier; missing or poorly installed insulation will fail
  • Fire clearances verified: 3" separation from recessed lights and heat-producing devices
  • Inspectors will sometimes inspect fire blocking if it was missed on the rough building inspection

Best Practices

  • R-19 fiberglass alone in a 2×6 wall does not meet the R-20 code minimum. Plan for mineral wool R-23, dense-pack cellulose, or add continuous insulation
  • Use EPS, not XPS or polyiso, below slabs because EPS doesn't absorb water; the others do and stop insulating
  • Don't confuse foil-faced polyiso (ASTM E84 compliant, can be left exposed) with EPS foam with mylar facing (not compliant)
  • Air seal before you insulate because insulation slows heat transfer but does not stop air movement; unsealed penetrations let conditioned air escape regardless of R-value
  • Photograph insulation before drywall because this is the last chance to document what's in the walls and ceilings
  • Insulate crawl space walls before framing begins above because it's much harder to access later
  • Coordinate with electrician on IC-rated recessed light locations vs. non-IC-rated. IC-rated fixtures can have insulation in direct contact; non-IC-rated require the 3" clearance

Client Communication

  • Explain the difference between R-value (thermal resistance) and air sealing; both are required for a comfortable, efficient home
  • If spray foam is chosen, advise that the house will need to be vacated during application and for the curing period specified by the product
  • Discuss upgrade options early: mineral wool for sound control between floors, closed-cell foam for basement rim joists, continuous exterior insulation for thermal bridging
