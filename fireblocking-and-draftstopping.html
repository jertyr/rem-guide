Fireblocking and Draftstopping

Fireblocking and draftstopping are building code requirements designed to slow the spread of fire and smoke through concealed spaces in wood-framed construction. Think of them as internal speed bumps that give occupants more time to escape and firefighters more time to respond. Fireblocking cuts off vertical draft paths in walls and other concealed spaces, while draftstopping divides large horizontal areas like attics and floor cavities into smaller compartments.

These measures are distinct from firestopping, which refers to protecting penetrations in fire-rated assemblies like garage walls or townhouse separation walls. All three are required by the 2015 Michigan Residential Code and 2021 International Residential Code.

Prerequisites

Fireblocking is installed after framing is complete and mechanical rough-ins are drilled, but before insulation. You need clear access to stud bays, floor cavities, and other concealed spaces. The work should be photographed before insulation is installed for inspector and client records.

Materials / Tools Needed

  • 2× sawn lumber cut-offs
  • Two 1× boards with lap joint (alternative to 2× lumber)
  • ¾" plywood or OSB
  • ½" gypsum board (drywall)
  • ¼" cement millboard or ⅜" particleboard
  • Mineral wool or unfaced fiberglass batts (must be packed tight)
  • Orange "fireblock" polyurethane foam (one or two component)
  • Red intumescent firestop caulk or collars (for rated assemblies only)
  • Framing nails, screws
  • Saw, hammer or drill
  • Camera for documentation

Process

  1. Identify locations requiring fireblocking - Walk the framing and mark every location where fireblocking is required (see "Where to Install Fireblocking" section below).
  2. Install solid fireblocking materials - Cut 2× lumber, plywood, or drywall to fit snugly between framing members. Material should be flush with framing faces and tightly fitted to prevent gaps.
  3. Use mineral wool or fiberglass where solid blocking is impractical - Pack the material tightly to completely fill the cavity. Loose or poorly packed insulation does not meet code.
  4. Seal top and bottom plate penetrations - Wherever pipes, cables, or ducts pass through top or bottom plates, seal around them with orange fireblock foam or mineral wool.
  5. Install draftstops in large horizontal cavities - Divide attics, soffits, and open-web floor systems into sections of 1,000 square feet or less using drywall or OSB.
  6. Apply firestop caulk or collars to rated penetrations - In garage walls, townhouse separation walls, or other fire-rated assemblies, use red intumescent firestop products per the listed UL system. Do not use orange fireblock foam for this purpose.
  7. Photograph all work - Take detailed photos of every fireblocking location before insulation covers the work. These photos serve as documentation for the inspector and as records for the homeowner.

Where to Install Fireblocking

Code requires fireblocking in the following locations:

  • At ceiling and floor levels - Block stud bays at the top and bottom plates where walls meet floors or ceilings.
  • Every 10 feet vertically - In walls taller than 10 feet, install horizontal blocking to divide the cavity into sections no more than 10 feet tall.
  • At soffits and dropped ceilings - Block the cavity between the soffit framing and the wall studs to prevent vertical draft.
  • At stair stringers - Block the triangular cavities between stair stringers and the wall.
  • Between floors in multi-story construction - Block where floor joists meet exterior walls and at the rim joist.
  • At connections between horizontal and vertical spaces - Any location where a concealed vertical space (like a stud bay) connects to a concealed horizontal space (like a floor joist cavity).
  • Around chimneys and fireplaces - Block the space between framing and masonry chimneys.
  • At wall and partition intersections - Where walls meet, block to prevent fire spread between cavities.

Approved Materials

The following materials are approved for fireblocking and draftstopping per the building code:

Material Code Reference
2× sawn lumber R302.11.1 #1
Two 1× boards with lap joint #2
¾" plywood or OSB #3
½" gypsum board #5
¼" cement millboard #6
Mineral wool or fiberglass batts (tightly packed) #7
Orange "fireblock" polyurethane foam* R302.11 Item 4
Red intumescent firestop caulk/collars** R302.4

*Foam must be specifically labeled as "fireblock" foam. Check the can before purchasing.

**Firestop products are only for rated assemblies and must be tested to ASTM E814 or UL 1479. Orange fireblock foam is NOT a substitute for red firestop caulk.

Garage Separation Requirements

Garages attached to the house have special fire separation requirements beyond standard fireblocking:

  • Garage walls: Minimum ½" gypsum board (drywall) on the garage side of all walls separating the garage from the house or its attic.
  • Garage ceiling with habitable space above: Minimum ⅝" Type X gypsum board on the garage ceiling.
  • Doors between garage and house: Minimum 1⅜" thick solid wood, solid core, or honeycomb steel door, or 20-minute fire-rated door. Door must be self-closing or automatic-closing.
  • Penetrations: All penetrations through garage separation walls (pipes, ducts, wires) must be sealed with listed firestop materials per R302.4.

Best Practices

  • Plan ahead: Add fireblocking to your framing checklist and walk the frame with your framer before rough-ins begin to identify all locations.
  • Use scrap lumber: Fireblocking is a great use for framing cut-offs. Keep a pile of short 2× pieces on site for this purpose.
  • Pre-rock soffits: Install drywall strips in dropped soffits during framing rather than trying to retrofit blocking later.
  • Mark your foam: Only use orange foam labeled specifically as "fireblock" or "fire blocking." Regular expanding foam does not meet code.
  • Don't confuse fireblock and firestop: Orange fireblock foam is for concealed cavities. Red firestop caulk is for rated assemblies. They are not interchangeable.
  • Photograph everything: Take photos before insulation. Inspectors appreciate documentation, and it protects you if questions arise later.
  • Coordinate with trades: Fireblocking is easier before plumbers and electricians drill holes everywhere. Install what you can early, then seal penetrations after rough-ins.

Common Issues & How to Avoid Them

  • Dropped soffits left unblocked: This is one of the most commonly missed items. Install drywall strips or solid blocking where soffits meet walls during framing, not after drywall is hung.
  • Using wrong foam in rated walls: Orange fireblock foam is not approved for fire-rated assemblies like garage walls. Use red intumescent firestop caulk or collars per the listed system.
  • Forgetting draftstops in open-web floor trusses: Floor trusses over basements or crawl spaces create large horizontal cavities that must be divided into 1,000 square foot sections. Add this to your framing checklist.
  • Loose-packed insulation: Fiberglass or mineral wool only works as fireblocking if it's tightly packed to completely fill the cavity. Inspectors will call this out if it's loose.
  • Missing plate penetrations: Every pipe, wire, and duct that passes through a top or bottom plate needs to be sealed. Walk the job with orange foam in hand after rough-ins are complete.
  • No documentation: If you don't photograph the work before insulation, you'll have no proof it was done correctly. Take the time to document.

Inspections

Fireblocking and draftstopping are inspected during the rough framing inspection, which cannot occur until after all rough-ins (plumbing, electrical, HVAC) are complete. The rough framing inspection happens before insulation is installed.

Some electrical and plumbing inspectors will check fireblocking during their inspections, but the primary inspection occurs at rough framing. If fireblocking is missing during rough framing, you may be able to complete it and have the inspector verify on the insulation inspection that follows, but this varies by jurisdiction.

Inspectors will pay special attention to:

  • Garage common walls and ceilings (R302.6)
  • Open-web floor trusses over finished basements
  • Stair stringers and the cavities around them (R302.7)
  • Soffit and dropped ceiling chases
  • Top and bottom plate penetrations
  • Walls over 10 feet tall (must have mid-height blocking)
  • Proper materials used (solid blocking, tight-packed insulation, labeled foam)

Client Communication

Fireblocking is a safety requirement that protects the homeowner and their family by slowing fire spread and giving more time to escape. Most clients don't think about this aspect of construction, but it's worth explaining why the work is important and showing them the photos of completed fireblocking before insulation goes in.

Some clients may question why you're spending time and money on something they can't see in the finished product. Explain that building codes exist to protect life safety, and fireblocking is one of the most important invisible systems in their home. It's not optional, and cutting corners here puts everyone at risk.
