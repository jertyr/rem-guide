Countertops

Countertop work covers templating, fabrication, and installation of quartz surfaces for kitchens, bathrooms, and other stone elements throughout the project. The builder controls the scheduling handoff: calling for the template at the right time, having the sink on site, and coordinating the 10–15 day fabrication window. This page focuses on quartz (engineered stone), which is the standard residential material, plus the other stone pieces that get coordinated alongside countertops.

Quick Reference

  • Call for template appointment right after cabinets are installed (sometimes after base cabinets only)
  • Sink must be on site for template; the countertop company will take it or measure it
  • Typical timeline: template → 10–15 days fabrication → install
  • Quartz is engineered stone (resin + ground quartz); not the same as quartzite (natural stone)
  • No hot pans directly on quartz; it will scald and yellow permanently
  • Clean with water and a rag; Goo Gone is OK for tough spots. DO NOT USE GOOF OFF
  • Discuss veining direction and slab approval with client before fabrication

Quartz vs. Quartzite

These are frequently confused by clients and sometimes by trades. They are completely different materials with opposite maintenance and heat characteristics.

  • Quartz (engineered): ~90% ground quartz particles + ~10% resins, pigments, and binders. Manufactured product with consistent color and pattern. Nonporous; never needs sealing. Heat-sensitive (resin can scorch above ~250°F)
  • Quartzite (natural stone): 100% natural metamorphic rock. Unique veining; no two slabs alike. Extremely heat-resistant. Requires sealing 1–2 times per year. Harder than granite
  • This guide covers quartz (engineered) unless otherwise noted

Prerequisites

  • Cabinets installed, leveled, and secured; at minimum, base cabinets must be in place for kitchen template
  • Sink on site; the countertop company will take it with them or measure it during template
  • Faucet and cooktop selected (not necessarily on site); hole locations and dimensions needed for template
  • Edge profile selected and confirmed with client
  • Slab selected and approved; discuss veining direction with client and determine if client wants to sign off on the exact slab drawing including vein placement
  • Backsplash height and details confirmed (if stone backsplash)
  • All other stone pieces identified: niche shelves, door thresholds, shower curbs, bench seats, shaving shelves

Template and Fabrication Workflow

  • Standard Workflow (Template → Fabricate → Install)
    • Call countertop company for template appointment once cabinets are installed
    • Templater arrives with digital laser measuring system; scans all surfaces, captures wall irregularities, and records dimensions to CAD software
    • Confirm all cutout locations (sink, faucet, cooktop, soap dispenser, outlets) during template
    • Confirm overhang dimensions, seam locations, and edge profile
    • Discuss veining direction; for non-homogeneous materials, determine whether client wants to approve the actual slab layout drawing before fabrication begins
    • Fabrication takes 10–15 business days after template
    • Countertop company returns to install finished pieces
  • Build-to-Print (Shop Drawing → Ship → Self-Install)
    • Builder provides a dimensioned shop drawing to the countertop company
    • Company fabricates from the drawing and ships to site or to the builder directly
    • Builder installs; typically used for bathroom vanities with a single sink
    • Tricky to get right. Measurements must be precise, and there is no room for field adjustment like a templated piece
    • Verify dimensions multiple times before submitting; include sink model, faucet hole spacing, and edge profile on the drawing

Other Stone Pieces

Besides countertops, the following items are often fabricated from the same or coordinating stone and should be included in the countertop scope or ordered alongside it:

  • Bathroom niche shelves (shower niches)
  • Door thresholds (bathroom entries)
  • Shower curbs / thresholds
  • Shower bench seats
  • Shaving shelves (shower)
  • Window sills (where stone is specified instead of wood)
  • Fireplace surrounds; less common because most countertop companies are reluctant due to heat concerns
    • Quartz should not be used near direct heat (gas fireplace inserts, wood-burning fireplaces); the resin will discolor and degrade
    • If the client wants stone at a fireplace, natural stone (granite, marble, quartzite) or porcelain slab is a better choice

Installation

  • Countertop company sets pieces on cabinets with silicone adhesive; seams are joined with color-matched epoxy
  • Undermount sinks are typically clipped and adhered from below during install
  • Support is critical for overhangs beyond 10–12"; steel brackets, corbels, or plywood subtops may be needed. Confirm with fabricator
  • Seam placement: fabricator plans seams during template; discuss visibility with client. Seams near sinks and cooktops are common due to slab size limits and cutout stress points
  • Plumber returns after install to connect sink, faucet, disposal, and dishwasher drain

Cleaning and Care

  • Daily cleaning: water and a soft rag; this is all that's needed for routine maintenance
  • If soap is needed: mild dish soap and water
  • Disinfecting: 70% isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol and water is the safest disinfectant for quartz
  • Stubborn stains or adhesive residue: use Goo Gone (citrus-based, mild); spot-test first, wipe clean after
  • DO NOT USE GOOF OFF: Goof Off contains acetone, xylene, and other harsh solvents that can damage the resin surface
    • Goo Gone = citrus oil + mild solvents → safe for quartz
    • Goof Off = acetone + xylene + ethylbenzene → too harsh for quartz
    • These products have similar names and are often shelved next to each other; make sure the crew grabs the right one
  • Do not set hot pans directly on the surface. Quartz resin scorches and yellows above approximately 250°F, and the damage is permanent. Always use trivets or hot pads
  • Safe: Dish soap, isopropyl alcohol, Goo Gone, Bar Keepers Friend (per Caesarstone), Soft Scrub Liquid Gel, 409, Fantastik, Windex
  • Avoid: Goof Off, acetone, bleach (discolors resin), vinegar (may etch resin over time), oven cleaners, abrasive pads, steel wool, paint strippers, toilet bowl cleaners
  • Quartz is nonporous; it never needs sealing (unlike granite or quartzite)

Best Practices

  • Get the sink on site before calling for the template; the countertop company will not template without it
  • For non-homogeneous quartz (visible veining), ask the client whether they want to approve the slab layout drawing before fabrication; this prevents disputes about vein direction and matching
  • For homogeneous / uniform-color quartz, slab approval is usually not necessary
  • Build-to-print is cost-effective for simple vanity tops but requires very accurate measurements; double-check before submitting
  • Identify all stone pieces early (niches, thresholds, curbs, benches, shelves) so they can be cut from the same slab or batch for color consistency
  • Don't use quartz for fireplace surrounds near direct heat; recommend natural stone or porcelain instead
  • Protect installed countertops immediately; cover with ram board or cardboard during remaining construction and tape edges to prevent debris from getting under the overhang
  • Schedule the plumber to return for sink/faucet hookup shortly after countertop install; the kitchen is not functional without a working sink

Client Communication

  • Explain the timeline: template after cabinets → 10–15 days fabrication → install day; this is one of the longer lead-time items in the finish phase
  • Quartz vs. quartzite: make sure the client understands which product they're getting; they have opposite heat and maintenance characteristics
  • Veining approval: ask early whether the client wants to see and approve the exact slab layout; some clients care deeply about vein direction and matching across seams
  • Heat damage: emphasize that hot pans, slow cookers, and curling irons will permanently scorch quartz; always use trivets
  • Cleaning: water is all that's needed day-to-day; provide the Goo Gone vs. Goof Off distinction at turnover since these get confused constantly
  • Seams: explain that seams are normal and their placement is determined by slab size and cutout locations; they will be color-matched and nearly invisible but not completely undetectable
  • Warranty: most fabricators exclude heat damage, chemical damage, and impact chips from warranty coverage; communicate this at turnover
