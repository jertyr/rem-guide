Flooring

Covers wood (engineered and solid) and luxury vinyl plank (LVP). Flooring goes in after drywall and priming with HVAC running. Doors are not installed until flooring is down. For tile floors, showers, and backsplashes, see Tile.

Quick Reference

  • HVAC running and conditioning the building before flooring begins
  • Doors installed after flooring, not before
  • Check moisture content of subfloor and flooring material before install
  • Acclimate wood flooring on site 1–2 weeks before install
  • Distribute flooring throughout the house, not all in one spot
  • Wood flooring runs perpendicular to joists; if parallel, add a second layer of OSB or cross-blocking
  • Vapor retarder required under wood flooring (0.7–50 perm rating)
  • Plan transitions between flooring types early; height differences need the right profile

Prerequisites

  • Drywall hung, finished, and primed
  • HVAC operating and conditioning the building
  • Moisture content checked on subfloor and drywall; use a dehumidifier if needed
  • Flooring delivered and acclimating on site 1–2 weeks; plan ahead as this often overlaps with drywall finishing
  • Distribute flooring throughout the house (concentrated weight can damage joists)
  • Subfloor clean, swept, and free of drywall mud, screws, or debris
  • Subfloor fastened tight; walk the floor and screw down any squeaks or loose areas before install
  • Doors not yet installed

Engineered Wood Flooring

Plywood backer with a hardwood veneer. Some are thin prefinished veneers; others are thicker veneers that can be prefinished or sand-in-place.

  • Vapor retarder required: perm rating 0.7–50 (silicone, tar paper, or roll-on options)
  • Run perpendicular to joists; if parallel, re-sheet with a second layer of OSB or add cross-blocking
  • Fastening, planks 3" wide or narrower:
    • Moisture content within 4 points of subfloor
    • 1.5" narrow crown staples (⅜" crown) every 3–4", or 1.25" cleats every 4–6"
  • Fastening, planks wider than 3":
    • Moisture content within 2 points of subfloor
    • 1.5" narrow crown staples (⅜" crown) every 3–4", or 1.25" cleats every 4–6"

Solid Wood Flooring

Common species in older homes: red oak, white oak, beech, maple. Typical new installs: red oak, white oak, maple, hickory.

  • Vapor retarder required: perm rating 0.7–50
  • Run perpendicular to joists; if parallel, re-sheet with a second layer of OSB or add cross-blocking
  • Fastening, planks 3" wide or narrower:
    • Moisture content within 4 points of subfloor
    • Two ⅜" crown staples or cleats every 8–10"
    • Minimum two fasteners per board, within 1–3" of each end
  • Fastening, planks wider than 3":
    • Moisture content within 2 points of subfloor
    • Two ⅜" crown staples or cleats every 6–8"
    • Minimum two fasteners per board, within 1–3" of each end

Sand-in-Place

  • Sand, stain, and finish on site after install
  • Approve stain samples on the actual species being installed, not a sample board of different wood
  • Plan 3–5 days for sanding, staining, and multiple coats of finish with dry time between each

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

Available in many sizes and styles. Often chosen for durability and water resistance.

  • Install methods:
    • Floating: Planks interlock but are not attached to the subfloor
    • Glue-down: Planks interlock and are glued to the subfloor. Better when subfloor is not perfectly flat because adhesion prevents hollow voids that cause clicks and separation
  • Subfloor must be flat. Check with a straightedge; NWFA tolerance is 3/16" in 10' for floating, tighter for glue-down
  • Level low spots with floor patch or self-leveling compound; grind down high spots
  • Follow manufacturer temperature requirements. Most LVP requires install and ongoing conditions between 65°F and 85°F
  • Leave expansion gaps at walls per manufacturer spec (typically ¼"–⅜"), covered by baseboard or shoe mold

Best Practices

  • Moisture mismatch between subfloor and flooring causes cupping, buckling, and gaps. Always check before install
  • Wider planks (over 3") require tighter moisture tolerance (within 2 points vs. 4 points) and closer fastener spacing
  • Protect finished floors immediately after install. Use ram board or similar; no bare cardboard (traps moisture) and no thin plastic alone (tears and lets debris through)
  • Leave expansion gaps at all walls and fixed objects for wood and LVP. These materials move with humidity changes
  • Plan transition strips between different flooring types early. Height differences between tile, wood, and LVP need the right profile and the subfloor may need to be built up or routed down to match
