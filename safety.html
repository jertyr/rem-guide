Jobsite Safety

What belongs on every jobsite to keep a crew safe and meet OSHA requirements. Citations point to the federal construction standard (29 CFR 1926) so you can trace any item back to the rule; state and local rules can be stricter.

Required PPE and Safety Equipment

Personal Protective Equipment

  • Z87 safety glasses (1926.102): ANSI Z87.1, marked Z87 on the frame or lens, Z87+ for high impact.
  • Hard hat (1926.100): ANSI Z89.1. Exposure based, not always required; see When Is a Hard Hat Required?
  • Hearing protection (1926.101, 1926.52): required at a 90 dBA 8-hour average, made available at 85 dBA.
  • N95 masks (1910.134): general dust, 95% of non-oil particles. Voluntary use still requires giving workers Appendix D.
  • P100 respirator (1910.134, 1926.1153): half-mask, 99.97% oil-proof, for silica, lead, and mold. Required use triggers a written program, medical evaluation, and annual fit test.
  • Work gloves (1926.95): cut-resistant for blades and sheet goods, chemical for solvents and finishes.
  • Foot protection (1926.96): protective footwear meeting ASTM F2413.
  • High-visibility vest (1926.201): for flaggers and anyone exposed to traffic or moving equipment.

Site Equipment

  • First aid kit (1926.50): stocked to crew size, ANSI/ISEA Z308.1 for contents.
  • Fire extinguisher (1926.150(c)): 2A minimum per 3,000 sq. ft., within 100 feet travel. A 10B within 50 feet of more than 5 gallons of flammable liquid or 5 lbs of flammable gas.
  • GFCI-protected cords (1926.404(b)(1)): all temporary 120V 15- and 20-amp receptacles GFCI protected, or run an assured equipment grounding conductor program.
  • Posted emergency contacts (1926.50(f)): physician, hospital, and ambulance numbers posted where 911 is not the sole contact.
  • Electrical pen tester (1926.416): verify a circuit is dead before opening a box or cutting into a wall.

Documentation On Site

  • Safety Data Sheets (SDS, formerly MSDS) (1910.1200, via 1926.59): accessible for every hazardous chemical on site. Electronic access is fine with a backup.
  • Written safety plan / accident prevention program (1926.20(b)(1)): see below.
  • Emergency action plan (1926.35): evacuation routes, reporting, accounting for workers.

When Is a Hard Hat Required?

Head protection is required where there is a possible danger of head injury from impact, falling or flying objects, or electrical shock (1926.100). No overhead work and no exposure means none is required; the moment another trade works above, it is back in play.

  • Required below overhead work, near crane or lift operations, in excavations with loads passing overhead, around exposed energized conductors, and in low-clearance framing
  • ANSI Z89.1: Type I for top impact, Type II adds lateral impact
  • Class E (electrical, 20kV), Class G (general, 2.2kV), Class C (conductive, no electrical protection)

A blanket site policy is the simplest way to meet the requirement across the whole site.

Written Safety Plan and Emergency Contacts

OSHA 1926.20(b) requires the employer to maintain an accident prevention program and designate competent persons to run it. Keep it specific to how the company works.

  • Designated safety coordinator / competent person (1926.20(b)(2), 1926.32(f))
  • Hazard communication program and where SDS sheets are accessed (1910.1200)
  • Required PPE by task and who provides it
  • Emergency action plan: nearest hospital, evacuation, injury reporting, severe weather (1926.35)
  • Posted emergency contacts at each active site (1926.50(f))
  • Trade-specific plans: silica exposure control (1926.1153(g)), fall protection, respiratory protection (1910.134)

Site Safety Walks and Inspections

OSHA 1926.20(b)(2) requires frequent and regular jobsite inspections by a competent person. Document them on the Site Safety Inspection Form as evidence of an active program.

  • Walk the site on a regular cadence, not just after a problem
  • The walker is the competent person: can identify hazards and is authorized to correct them (1926.32(f))
  • Log the hazard, the corrective action, and the date closed

Training and Certifications

Equipment without training does not meet the requirement. Keep records of who is trained and when.

  • First aid / CPR (1926.50(c)): a trained responder on site where no medical facility is near; Red Cross, AHA, or equivalent, renewed about every two years.
  • Respirator fit test and medical evaluation (1910.134(e), (f)): medical evaluation, then a fit test for the specific make, model, and size; refit at least annually; clean-shaven seal area.
  • Silica awareness (1926.1153(i)): for cutting, grinding, or drilling concrete, masonry, tile, or stone; covers hazards, controls, and Table 1.
  • Ladder safety (1926.1060): competent-person training; 4:1 setup and 3 feet above the landing.
  • Safety coordinator / competent person (1926.20(b), 1926.32(f)): runs the program, inspects, and has authority to stop work. Many standards each require a competent person.
