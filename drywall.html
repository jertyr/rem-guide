Drywall

Covers stocking, hanging, and finishing. Can be a finish material or a base for additional surface coverings.

Quick Reference

  • Insulation installed and inspected before drywall
  • Distribute sheets throughout the house, not all in one room
  • Glue and screw; screws every 16" on 16 OC framing (2015 MRC 702.3.5)
  • Seams in the middle of a header or away from it, never at the corners
  • Screw inspection required between hanging and finishing in some jurisdictions
  • Corner bead installed after hanging, before taping
  • Pre-fill gaps and butt joints before taping
  • Cover furnace return air vents during sanding
  • Clean up dust and debris before priming; painter often handles prime

Materials

  • Standard drywall: 4×8 to 4×16 sheets of compressed gypsum with paper face. Also available in 54" wide sheets
    • ½" for most residential walls and ceilings with 16 OC framing
    • ⅝" required for fire-rated assemblies (Type X); sometimes used on ceilings with 24 OC framing to resist sag
    • ¼" and ⅜" for curved walls and layered applications
  • Moisture-resistant drywall for areas with intermittent moisture (laundry, utility rooms); not rated for direct water contact like shower surrounds
  • Densglass or similar fiberglass-faced sheathing for high-humidity areas and exterior applications; check local code for where required
  • Higher fire-rating drywall available beyond standard Type X where assemblies require it
  • Drywall adhesive, not construction adhesive (PL, Liquid Nails); use adhesive specifically labeled for drywall
  • Drywall screws: coarse thread for wood framing, fine thread for steel studs

Prerequisites

  • Site clean and swept
  • Plates cut out of door openings
  • Temporary nailers and blocks removed
  • Drywall backing in place at all inside corners, tub/shower flanges, and soffits
  • Confirm the sub can physically get sheets to all areas (trusses, corners, soffits)
  • Insulation installed and insulation inspection passed
  • Tub and shower units set (drywall lands on tub/shower flange)
  • Mark windows, doors, and openings that will be drywall wrapped
  • Start/stop points marked on framing (shower areas, tile surrounds, anywhere drywall stops short)

Process

  • Coordination
    • Drywaller does a site visit to measure and create a drywall list: sheet sizes, thicknesses, specialty boards (Type X, moisture-resistant)
    • Tell the sub where drywall can be stocked inside the home
  • Stocking and Delivery
    • Ordered from a drywall yard; yard delivers with a truck, crew, and boom
    • Yard will not carry drywall up or down stairs
      • Second floor: stock through a window, door, or other opening using the boom
      • Basement: stock through a walkout door or egress window opening
      • Builder may need to leave an opening enlarged or open a temporary hole in the second story
    • Distribute sheets throughout the house; don't stack all material in one room (floor load and workflow)
    • Lean sheets against walls with the finish (white) side facing out so the sub can read markings
  • Hanging
    • Glue to framing and screw off; screws every 16" when glued on 16 OC framing (2015 MRC 702.3.5)
    • Ceilings first, then walls. Hang perpendicular to framing where possible to span more members
    • Stagger joints; no four-corner intersections. Offset seams from adjacent sheets
    • Place seams in the middle of a header or away from it entirely. Seams at the corners of headers will crack
    • Drive screws slightly below paper surface without breaking paper face
    • Cut out for electrical boxes, plumbing access panels, and HVAC registers as you go
    • Leave ⅛" gap at floor. Drywall should not sit directly on subfloor or concrete
  • Screw Inspection
    • After hanging, before finishing
    • Inspector checks screw spacing, depth (dimpled but not through paper), edge distances, and that seams land on framing
  • Corner Bead
    • Install after hanging and before taping begins
    • Glue with spray adhesive; nail or staple if metal bead
  • Finishing (Tape and Mud)
    • Pre-fill gaps, butt joints, and deep seams with compound before taping
    • Field butt joints first so that tape is buried when corners are done
    • Tape coat: embed paper or mesh tape in joint compound over all seams and corners
    • Screw heads: mud only, no tape
    • Second coat (fill/block): wider pass of compound to fill and smooth seams
    • Third coat (skim/finish): feathered thin to blend seams invisible
    • Final sand after last coat
    • Inside corners: paper tape folded and set in compound
    • Outside corners: corner bead mudded and feathered
  • Finish Levels
    • Level 0: No taping or finishing (above ceilings in non-accessible areas)
    • Level 1: Tape embedded in compound, no additional coats (fire-rated areas, above ceilings, attics, garages)
    • Level 2: Tape + one coat compound (where tile or paneling covers the surface)
    • Level 3: Tape + two coats (heavy textured finishes)
    • Level 4: Tape + three coats (standard for paint with flat or light texture, most residential)
    • Level 5: Level 4 + skim coat over entire surface (critical lighting or gloss/semi-gloss paint where imperfections show)
  • Cleanup
    • Sweep and vacuum all drywall dust and debris from floors, windowsills, and ledges
    • Remove scrap drywall and empty compound buckets from the site
  • Prime
    • Prime right after finishing to seal dust and keep the site clean
    • Prime before stained trim. Drywall dust gets into wood grain and will not come out
    • Often the painter handles priming rather than the drywall sub

Best Practices

  • Confirm boom truck access before stocking is scheduled. Overhead wires, tight driveways, and tree limbs can prevent delivery
  • Photograph all rough-in wiring, plumbing, and ductwork before drywall covers it. Last chance to document what's in the walls
  • Provide heat during finishing. Joint compound will not cure properly in cold conditions
  • Run a dehumidifier to help compound dry between coats
  • Ventilate during finishing to reduce humidity
  • Don't rush finish coats. Drying time between coats prevents cracking, bubbling, and poor adhesion
  • Cover furnace return air vents during sanding. Drywall dust will get pulled into the HVAC system
