Plumbing Rough Install

Plumbing rough covers water supply, drain/waste/vent (DWV), and gas piping. The plumber may also rough in hot water or steam radiant systems. Tube types range from copper and PEX to iron pipe and PVC/ABS.

Quick Reference

  • Get rough-in valves, tubs, shower trays, and shower liners on site before the plumber starts
  • Mark toilet rough-in dimensions
  • Confirm drains don't land on joists or beams in the floor system below
  • Plan vent pipe paths through the roof
  • Mark cabinets and trim so valves and shutoffs clear finished elements

Prerequisites

  • Concrete slab removed or not yet poured—plumber needs access for underground drains
  • Framing complete; in remodels, subfloor may need to stay loose for drain install
  • Rough-in valves, tubs, showers, and shower pans on site
  • Toilet rough-in distances marked or specs available
  • Baseboard trim height marked where toilet goes
  • Vanity cabinets marked, especially floating cabinets and wall-hung or pedestal sinks
  • Centerlines for site-built tiled showers and shower head heights set

Materials and Tools

  • Fixtures: Shower valves, diverters, shower trays, tubs, shower surrounds
  • DWV: PVC/ABS pipe & fittings, test caps, solvent/cement, cleanout tees & plugs
  • Water supply: PEX/copper, fittings, manifolds, stub-out elbows/plates, valves, washing-machine/ice-maker boxes
  • Gas: Black iron/CSST, striker plates, unions/drip legs, test gauge
  • Protection: Tub/shower covers, nail plates, firestop caulk
  • Testing: Test plugs, pump

Layout and Coordination

  • Walk the site with the plumber: go over fixture locations and heights, vent paths, and joist bays to use or avoid
  • Mark centerlines and finished-floor (AFF) elevations; confirm tub drains, valve depth vs. tile thickness, and niche locations
  • Pull specs for hand showers and slide bars

Inspections

  • Plumbing Rough: Drains may need to be pressurized for leak testing; inspector checks slope, venting, cleanouts, supports, protection, and firestopping
  • Gas Pressure Test: New gas lines capped and pressurized; pressure gauge visible during inspection
  • Shower Pan: Flood test before tile or backer goes in, after waterproofing with Oatey liner, Schluter tray, or similar

Best Practices

  • Pull valve and trim cut sheets before rough; keep trims on site for the tilesetter. Confirm correct depth in wall—mock up a wall section if needed
  • Keep tubs and shower trays protected; leave space around flanges and drains when covering
  • Install shutoffs local to each fixture; group shutoffs by fixture cluster for easier service later
  • Make a story pole or mark the floor plate with tile thickness, backer, and centerline so the plumber doesn't have to guess
  • Keep nail plates and fire caulk on hand; do a firestop check before the inspector arrives
  • Leave the job inspection-ready: tidy, plans open, gauges visible, test levels marked

Client Communication

  • Let the client know there will be an interruption in water and drain service; they may need to avoid running water or flushing toilets
  • Photograph all rough-ins after installation
