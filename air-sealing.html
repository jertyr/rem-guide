Air Sealing

Air sealing is the continuous barrier that controls air movement through the building envelope. It is distinct from the vapor retarder layer, but on modern wall assemblies the two are often the same membrane. A tight envelope cuts heating and cooling loads, prevents condensation in cavities, and is what makes high-performance insulation actually perform. Aim for a verified blower-door result, not just a checklist.

This page covers the interior side of the assembly — smart membranes, tapes, and gaskets installed before insulation. Exterior weather sealing (WRBs, flashing) belongs with Windows, Exterior Doors, Roofing, and Siding & Trim.

Prerequisites

Air sealing is installed after framing, fireblocking, and rough trades (mech, plumbing, electrical) are complete and inspected, but before insulation. Penetrations should be marked, and the air-control layer (interior membrane, sheathing, or drywall) needs to be defined on the plans before work starts. Photograph everything before insulation goes in.

Choosing a Smart Vapor Membrane

Smart (variable-permeance) membranes act as an air barrier and a vapor retarder that opens up to allow inward drying when humidity rises in the cavity. They are the right choice for cold-climate (Zone 5+) walls and roofs with vapor-sensitive sheathing or dense-pack cellulose. The three commonly specified options:

How to choose:

  • MemBrain — thin polyamide film, simplest install, easiest to source. Pick this when the budget is tight or the project doesn’t justify a full proprietary system. You assemble your own tape/sealant kit.
  • Majrex 200 — reinforced sheet with strong tear/water-tightness data and a complete SIGA accessory line if you’re already using SIGA tapes on the exterior.
  • Intello Plus — reinforced membrane with the deepest accessory ecosystem (especially for penetrations and dense-pack). Best fit when the assembly has a lot of penetrations or you’re running a Passive House–adjacent build.

Tapes & Gaskets

The membrane is only as tight as the seams and penetrations. Match tape and gasket to the membrane manufacturer when possible — warranties and long-term adhesion data assume same-system installation. See the Products page for SKUs and links.

  • Seam tape: SIGA Rissan 60 (interior) or pro clima TESCON VANA. For MemBrain, 3M 8087CW is the common field choice.
  • Window/door interior tape: SIGA Fentrim IS 20.
  • Pre-formed corner pieces: SIGA Rissan 430 corner saves time on inside/outside corners at rough openings.
  • Pipe, cable, and conduit penetrations: SIGA Fentrim Grommets (or pro clima Roflex/Kaflex) sized to the penetration. Cover wires up through 4″ PVC and ERV vents.
  • Bottom plate to slab/subfloor: sill-seal foam gasket plus an acoustic/air-seal sealant under the plate before framing stand-up. After the fact, run a continuous bead of acoustic sealant along the bottom plate before drywall.
  • Outlet and switch boxes in air-barrier walls: 3M MPP+ moldable putty pads wrapped around old-work boxes, or use airtight boxes (Lessco, Allproof).

Process

  1. Define the air-control layer on plans. Decide whether the barrier is the interior membrane, the exterior sheathing (taped), or a hybrid. Mark the layer with a continuous color highlighter on a section drawing so every trade knows what they cannot puncture.
  2. Seal bottom plates and rim joists. Acoustic sealant under bottom plates, sill seal at slab/subfloor transitions, taped or sprayed seal at rim joists.
  3. Pre-seal box penetrations. Wrap outlet/switch boxes with putty pads or install airtight boxes before drywall.
  4. Hang the membrane horizontally. Run from top plate down, lap each course over the one below by at least the seam-tape width. Staple to studs every 6–8″ with cap staples; the staples themselves don’t need to be airtight.
  5. Tape every seam. Lap tape at least 2″. Roll the tape with a J-roller for full adhesion.
  6. Detail every penetration. Pipe and cable penetrations get the matching grommet; ducts get a flashing-tape transition. No bare staples or screws through the membrane without tape over them.
  7. Tape the membrane to surrounding assemblies. At top plates, exterior corners, window/door openings, and at the foundation/subfloor transition. Use the manufacturer’s transition tape (Fentrim IS 20 or TESCON VANA).
  8. Photograph the entire wall before insulation. Mark photos to plans.
  9. Verify with a blower-door test. Run a pre-drywall test before insulation if possible; it’s the only time leaks are easy to find. Re-test at final.

Where Air Sealing Is Required

The 2021 IRC and 2015 Michigan Residential Code list 16 air-sealing details under N1102.4 (R402.4), verified by visual inspection. The high-impact items:

  • General continuity of the air barrier across the entire envelope
  • Top plate to attic, including dropped soffits and chases
  • Knee walls and skylight shafts
  • Exterior wall corners, headers, and rim joists
  • Window and door rough openings
  • Recessed lights (must be IC–AT rated and gasketed)
  • Plumbing and wiring penetrations of top and bottom plates
  • Tubs and showers on exterior walls (rigid air barrier behind)
  • Electrical and communication boxes on exterior walls
  • HVAC register boots sealed to subfloor or drywall
  • Concealed access panels (attic hatches gasketed and weatherstripped)

Common Issues & How to Avoid Them

  • Tub / shower wall on an exterior wall: install rigid air barrier (drywall or rigid foam taped) behind the tub before the tub is set. After the tub is in, that bay is unreachable.
  • Dropped soffits and chases: seal the cavity at the framing stage, not after drywall.
  • Recessed cans: only IC–AT rated cans count as airtight; older non-rated cans are major leaks. Better: surface-mount LED disks on a sealed ceiling.
  • Membrane stapled but not taped: staples alone are not airtight. Every seam, lap, and penetration must be taped.
  • Wrong tape on MemBrain: not all contractor tapes adhere long-term to nylon. Use 3M 8087CW or a manufacturer-tested alternative.
  • Caulking instead of taping: latex caulks shrink and crack. Use acoustic sealant or proper air-sealing tape at moving joints.
  • Skipping the pre-drywall blower door: finding leaks after drywall is hung means cutting it open. A pre-drywall test is the cheapest way to fix problems.
