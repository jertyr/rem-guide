Exterior Siding & Trim

Siding and trim are the first line of defense against the weather. They keep most elements off the weather resistive barrier (WRB) while allowing drying if moisture gets behind. This guide covers common siding types including vinyl, fiber cement, LP SmartSide, wood lap, and shakes, along with trim options in wood, PVC, composite, and aluminum.

Quick Reference

  • WRB and flashing inspected/approved before starting
  • Layout established with reference lines snapped
  • Blind-nail on studs with correct fasteners and length
  • Seal all field cuts and end-prime wood products
  • Install cap flashing at all horizontal trim intersections
  • Leave manufacturer-specified expansion gaps
  • Use stainless fasteners for cedar

Prerequisites

  • Windows and doors installed and properly flashed
  • Continuous WRB installed, taped, and inspected if local "WRB/Siding" inspection required
  • Ledger boards installed and porches marked out
  • Lighting, outlet, spigot, mailbox, and address locations marked or trim blocks pre-installed
  • Material on site (see order sheet)

Types of Siding

Fiber Cement (Hardie)

Fiber cement is manufactured by several companies, with James Hardie being the primary product in most markets. It comes primed and must be painted. Pre-painted options exist but are difficult to install without damaging the finish.

  • Available in wood grain and smooth profiles
  • Durable and looks great once installed
  • Brittle, messy to cut, and harder to install than other options
  • Requires silica dust protection when cutting (N95 respirator minimum, P100 recommended)
  • Use fiber cement-specific saw blades or a guillotine shear to reduce dust

Tools: Fiber cement saw blades or guillotine shear, siding nailer (coil or strip), spray primer for cut edges, 15-gauge trim gun, N95/P100 respirator

Fasteners: Blind nail with siding nails 1" down from top edge, or along nail line. Minimum 1.25" penetration into studs or sheathing. 16" O.C. into studs; some codes allow sheathing nailing with specific nail types. Use hot-dipped galvanized or stainless steel.

LP SmartSide

LP SmartSide is an engineered wood siding made from wood strands bonded with resins and treated with zinc borate for rot and termite resistance. It's lighter than fiber cement and cuts with standard wood tools without silica dust concerns.

  • Comes pre-primed; must be painted within 180 days of installation
  • All cut edges must be field-primed before installation
  • Available in lap, panel, and shake profiles
  • 50-year limited warranty when installed per manufacturer specs
  • Easier to install than fiber cement but still requires careful handling

Tools: Standard wood cutting tools, siding nailer, spray primer for cut edges, 15-gauge trim gun

Fasteners: Minimum 0.113" shank diameter, hot-dipped galvanized. 1.5" minimum penetration into framing.

Wood Siding

Wood siding was historically the most common option and is found on older homes throughout North America. Older homes typically used softwood or cedar, which can last decades if kept painted. Cedar is now the primary species for exterior wood siding. Pine is acceptable for protected areas like porch ceilings that are not exposed to weather.

  • WindsorOne offers a 30-year exterior warranty if cut edges are primed before install and painted within one year using exterior acrylic latex (e.g., Sherwin Williams Emerald)
  • Cedar and other wood can attract insects and woodpeckers
  • Easy to install and repair compared to other materials

Tools: Standard wood cutting tools, siding nailer, spray primer for cut edges, 15-gauge trim gun

Fasteners: Cedar requires stainless steel ring-shank nails. 1.5" minimum penetration.

Vinyl

Vinyl is one of the most common siding types on new construction due to low cost and ease of installation. It does not require painting and comes in many colors and profiles including lap, shake, and specialty options.

  • Expected lifespan: 20-30 years
  • Must be installed loosely to allow thermal expansion
  • Do not nail tight; leave 1/32" gap at nail heads
  • Lock each course fully into the one below
  • Colors fade over time; darker colors show fading more

Tools: Snips, grinder, or circular saw with fine-tooth blade; J-channel, starter strip, utility trim; snap-lock punch for top courses

Fasteners: 1.5" roofing nails or equivalent. Nail in center of slots, not tight.

Types of Trim

Wood Trim

Cedar or WindsorOne can be installed as exterior trim. Wood is easy to install and repair but can attract insect pests and woodpeckers.

Tools: Standard wood cutting tools, 15-gauge trim gun, spray primer for cut edges

Boral TruExterior

Boral TruExterior is made of poly-ash (fly-ash and binders). It cuts and installs like wood but is brittle during handling. It profiles nicely with crisp edges, does not move much on the wall, can be painted any color including dark colors, and is completely waterproof.

Tools: Siding nailer, 15-gauge trim gun

PVC Trim (Azek)

Azek is the primary PVC trim option. It comes white and does not require painting.

  • Install with Cortex screws and matching plugs
  • PVC expands and contracts significantly; follow manufacturer gap requirements
  • Difficult to keep clean during and after installation
  • Smooth surfaces scratch easily
  • Can be painted dark colors only with high-reflectivity paints (e.g., Sherwin Williams VinylSafe)

Tools: Cortex screws and plugs, PVC solvent weld (cement), pocket hole jig and screws

Aluminum

Bent aluminum is often paired with vinyl siding. It comes in 2' x 50' rolls and is formed on site with a brake. Available in standard colors and does not need painting.

  • Can be installed hollow or over wood backing
  • Large flat areas will appear wavy even when installed correctly
  • Cannot be repaired once dented; must be replaced

Tools: Metal blades and angle grinder, snips, hand brake and bench brake, trim nails, pea shooter, narrow crown stapler

Process

For a complete materials list, please reference the material order sheet.

  • Determine Details or Mock-Up
    • Show client full-scale examples of trim sizes and styles
    • Photos, sketches, or renderings work if samples unavailable
    • Confirm corners, trim, friezes, base, windows, bands, and soffit details
  • Layout
    • Snap reference lines for bottom course and work up
    • Adjust reveals to hit windows and soffit/frieze lines cleanly, except with Vinyl
    • Mark stud locations if blind nailing
  • Trim First
    • Install corners, skirt, and frieze
    • Maintain manufacturer-specified gaps and back-prime cut ends
    • Screw and plug PVC trim
    • Consider pocket-hole screwing window trim from behind before installation
    • For vinyl: install J-channel and starter strip
    • Install drip cap and tape over horizontal trim
  • Install Siding
    • Fiber Cement Lap: Blind-nail 1" down from top, 16" O.C., flash seams, prime all cut edges
    • LP SmartSide and Wood Lap: Blind-nail similar to fiber cement, prime cut edges
    • Cedar: Use stainless steel fasteners exclusively
    • Vinyl: Lock each course, nail loosely in slot centers, maintain 1/4" gaps at trim, or as specified by manufacturer for install temp.
  • Caulk and Finish
    • Use high-quality sealant (Quad or equivalent)
    • Caulk body color against trim rather than trim color
    • PVC does not require painting, but if painted use high-reflectivity paint only

Rainscreen and Furring

Rainscreen systems provide a drainage plane and ventilation gap behind siding, significantly improving moisture management. Options include:

  • 1x3 or 1x4 vertical furring strips over WRB
  • Manufactured rainscreen products (e.g., Slicker, Home Slicker, Driwall)
  • Benjamin Obdyke HydroGap housewrap with built-in drainage
  • Required or recommended in high-moisture climates and over foam sheathing
  • Provides path for incidental water to drain and dry

Best Practices

  • Always back-prime cut edges on wood and fiber cement products before installation
  • Leave required expansion gaps per manufacturer specs; PVC and vinyl move significantly
  • Never paint PVC or vinyl dark colors unless using approved high-reflectivity paint
  • Install cap flashing above all horizontal trim pieces (window heads, band boards, friezes)
  • Maintain 8" minimum clearance from siding to grade (IRC R703.7.1)
  • Do not caulk the bottom edge of lap siding; it must drain
  • Wear appropriate PPE when cutting fiber cement (silica dust is hazardous)
  • Store siding flat and off the ground to prevent warping
  • Acclimate materials to site temperature before installation when possible
  • Flash all penetrations (lights, outlets, spigots) before installing siding around them

Inspections

  • WRB Inspection (required in some jurisdictions):
    • Inspector verifies WRB installed per manufacturer specifications
    • Tyvek: cap staples, proper laps, tape at seams (see instructions)
    • Zip System: taped seams with Zip tape, proper corner treatment
    • Window and door flashing integration verified
  • Some jurisdictions require final siding inspection:
    • Fastener pattern and penetration
    • Clearance to grade
    • Flashing at penetrations

Client Communication

  • Confirm color and style selections in writing before ordering
  • Discuss maintenance requirements: repainting schedules, cleaning methods, caulk inspection
  • Warn about potential pest issues (insects, woodpeckers) with wood and some composite products
  • Advise that landscaping around the home may be damaged during installation
  • Explain that repair patches with new material may not match weathered existing siding
  • Provide expected lifespan: vinyl 20-30 years, fiber cement 30-50 years, wood 30+ years with maintenance, LP SmartSide 30-50 years
