Flooring

Flooring covers wood (engineered and solid), luxury vinyl plank (LVP), and tile installation. Flooring goes in after drywall and priming, with HVAC operating to condition the building. Doors are not installed until after flooring is down. This page covers material options, moisture and acclimation requirements, fastening specs, tile coordination with the shower waterproofing and flood test, and the order/checklist templates that keep installs on track.

Quick Reference

  • HVAC must be running and conditioning the building before flooring begins
  • Do not install doors until after flooring is in
  • Check moisture content of subfloor, drywall, and flooring before install
  • Acclimate wood flooring on site 1–2 weeks before install
  • Don't stack all flooring in one spot — distribute the weight across joists
  • Wood flooring runs perpendicular to joists; if parallel, add a second layer of OSB or cross-blocking
  • Install wood flooring over a vapor retarder (0.7–50 perm rating)
  • Tile shower flood test is scheduled as a plumbing inspection — coordinate timing with plumber and tilesetter

Prerequisites

  • Drywall hung, finished, and primed
  • HVAC operating and conditioning the building
  • Check moisture content of subfloor and drywall; use a dehumidifier if needed to bring levels down
  • Flooring delivered and acclimating on site 1–2 weeks (often difficult right after drywall — plan ahead)
  • Distribute flooring throughout the house — don't stack all material in one spot (weight can damage joists)
  • Subfloor clean, swept, and free of drywall mud, screws, or debris
  • Subfloor fastened tight — walk the floor and screw down any squeaks or loose areas before install
  • Doors not yet installed (install doors after flooring)

Engineered Wood Flooring

Plywood backer with a hardwood veneer. Some are thin prefinished veneers; others are thicker veneers that can be prefinished or sand-in-place.

  • Vapor retarder required: perm rating 0.7–50 (silicone, tar paper, or roll-on options)
  • Run perpendicular to joists; if parallel, re-sheet with a second layer of OSB or add cross-blocking
  • Fastening — planks 3" wide or narrower:
    • Moisture content within 4 points of subfloor
    • 1.5" narrow crown staples (⅜" crown) every 3–4", or 1.25" cleats every 4–6"
  • Fastening — planks wider than 3":
    • Moisture content within 2 points of subfloor
    • 1.5" narrow crown staples (⅜" crown) every 3–4", or 1.25" cleats every 4–6"

Solid Wood Flooring

Common species in older homes: red oak, white oak, beech, maple. Typical new installs: red oak, white oak, maple, hickory.

  • Vapor retarder required: perm rating 0.7–50
  • Run perpendicular to joists; if parallel, re-sheet with a second layer of OSB or add cross-blocking
  • Fastening — planks 3" wide or narrower:
    • Moisture content within 4 points of subfloor
    • Two ⅜" crown staples or cleats every 8–10"
    • Minimum two fasteners per board, within 1–3" of each end
  • Fastening — planks wider than 3":
    • Moisture content within 2 points of subfloor
    • Two ⅜" crown staples or cleats every 6–8"
    • Minimum two fasteners per board, within 1–3" of each end

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

Available in many sizes and styles; often chosen for durability and water resistance/waterproof properties.

  • Install methods:
    • Floating: Planks interlock but are not attached to the subfloor
    • Glue-down: Planks interlock and are glued to the subfloor — better when subfloor is not flat, because adhesion prevents hollow voids that can cause separation
  • Subfloor must be flat — check with a 10' straightedge; NWFA tolerance is 3/16" in 10' for floating, tighter for glue-down
  • Level low spots with floor patch or self-leveling compound; grind down high spots
  • Follow manufacturer temperature requirements — most LVP requires install and ongoing conditions between 65°F and 85°F
  • Leave expansion gaps at walls per manufacturer spec (typically ¼"–⅜") — covered by baseboard or shoe mold

Tile

Common on laundry and bath floors, custom showers, kitchen backsplashes, and decorative locations. Tile comes in many sizes, shapes, and thicknesses — plan selections early because they affect layout, substrate prep, and transition details.

  • Planning
    • Confirm tile selections, pattern, and layout early
    • Plan thresholds, edges, profiles, grout color, and caulk color
    • Plan niches: size, location, and tile for niche interior
    • Confirm shower walls are plumb, square, and flat before tile prep begins
  • Shower Coordination
    • Builder may leave drywall out of shower area for tilesetter to prep, or prep the shower before drywall so sheetrock can tie into the waterproofing for a single flat surface
    • Install shower pan and drain; protect the pan during subsequent work
    • Follow manufacturer instructions for waterproofing products (Schluter Kerdi, Laticrete Hydro Ban, etc.)
  • Substrate Prep
    • Clean framing/subfloor; confirm flat, level, plumb, and square
    • Floors may need leveling compound — prime substrate with tile primer first
    • Mechanically fasten wire mesh for wood-floor leveler pours
    • Use spray foam to dam leveler at edges; tape seams so leveler doesn't leak through
    • Install decoupling membrane on floors (e.g., Schluter Ditra) to prevent tile cracking from seasonal framing movement
  • Install
    • Install wall tile after shower pan is set and inspected
    • Set tile with appropriate thinset for the substrate and tile type (modified vs. unmodified per membrane manufacturer requirements)
    • Back-butter large-format tiles (any dimension over 15") for full coverage
    • Seal natural stone before grouting
    • Grout joints; caulk all change-of-plane transitions (wall-to-floor, wall-to-wall corners) with color-matched sanded caulk

Tile Flood Test and Inspection

  • Walk-in showers require a flood test — this is scheduled as a plumbing inspection even though the tilesetter does the waterproofing
  • Builder must coordinate timing between plumber, tilesetter, and inspector
  • Tilesetter plugs the drain and fills the pan with 2" of water before inspection
  • After inspection passes, tilesetter continues with wall tile and floor tile

Tile Prep Tools

  • Wet saw (correct blade for tile type) and tile snapper
  • Angle grinder with hole saws, finger bit, cone, and polishing pads
  • Grinding sponges
  • Screeds and straightedges
  • Buckets, sponges, shop towels
  • Trowels (correct notch size for tile)
  • Mixing paddle
  • Hose
  • Laser level and tripod
  • Leveling clips and spacers/shims
  • Plastic, tape, and floor protection
  • Tub protection

Order Sheets and Checklists

  • Tile Prep Order Sheet (Schluter products)
    • Ditra / Ditra XL / Ditra Heat membrane
    • Ditra Heat cable kit and thermostat
    • Kerdi board, screws, and washers
    • Kerdi band and Kerdi rolls
    • Kerdi seals (pipe and mixing valve)
    • Kerdi Fix sealant
    • Niches
    • Inside and outside corners
    • All-Set thinset
  • Tile Checklist
    • Layout pattern confirmed
    • Grout color and type selected
    • Caulk color selected
    • Edge profiles and transitions confirmed
    • Takeoff complete (sq ft + waste)
  • Plank Floor Order Sheet
    • Square footage + waste percentage
    • Number of boxes
    • Nosings and transitions
    • Underlayment / vapor retarder
    • Fasteners (staples or cleats)
    • Adhesive (if glue-down)
    • Leveler and primer
    • Stain (if sand-in-place)

Best Practices

  • Check moisture content of both the subfloor and the flooring material before install — moisture mismatch causes cupping, buckling, and gaps
  • Acclimate wood flooring on site 1–2 weeks; store flat, off the ground, and distributed across the house
  • Wider planks (over 3") require tighter moisture tolerance (within 2 points vs. 4 points) and closer fastener spacing
  • Run wood flooring perpendicular to joists — if you must run parallel, add a second layer of OSB or cross-blocking to prevent deflection
  • Glue-down LVP is better than floating on subfloors that aren't perfectly flat — floating installs leave voids that cause clicks and separation
  • Coordinate tile shower timing: the flood test is a plumbing inspection, so the plumber needs to be involved even though the tilesetter does the work
  • Seal natural stone before grouting — grout will stain unsealed stone permanently
  • Protect finished floors immediately after install — use ram board or similar; no bare cardboard (traps moisture) and no thin plastic alone (tears and lets debris through)
  • Leave expansion gaps at all walls and fixed objects for wood and LVP — these materials move with humidity changes

Client Communication

  • Doors are installed after flooring — explain why (door bottoms are scribed to finished floor height)
  • Wood floors move with seasonal humidity: explain that gaps in winter and tightness in summer are normal for solid and some engineered products
  • Discuss finish expectations for sand-in-place floors: stain samples should be approved on the actual species being installed, not a sample board of a different wood
  • Tile grout is not waterproof — explain that shower waterproofing is behind the tile (membrane), not in the grout joints; caulk at change-of-plane joints needs periodic replacement
  • LVP is water-resistant on the surface but water can still get under floating floors through edges and seams — it is not a substitute for proper waterproofing in wet areas
  • Acclimation takes 1–2 weeks and must happen before install — build this into the schedule
