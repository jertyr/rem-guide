Jobsite Safety Signage

Signage is the cheapest way to warn people of a hazard and to show an inspector you flagged it. OSHA 1926.200 requires accident prevention signs and tags wherever needed to warn of hazards, and it adopts the ANSI Z535 color and format system. The goal here is simple: know which signs belong on a site, get them from a reliable source or print your own, and post the emergency information OSHA actually requires.

Signal Word and Color System

Match the signal word to the level of hazard. Using the right word and color is what makes a sign defensible (1926.200, ANSI Z535).

Signal wordColorUse when
DANGERRedImmediate hazard that will cause death or serious injury
WARNINGOrangeHazard that could cause death or serious injury
CAUTIONYellowHazard that could cause minor or moderate injury
NOTICEBluePolicy or information, not a personal-injury hazard
SAFETY / FIRST AIDGreenLocation of safety equipment, first aid, exits

Signs to Have on Site

Site Entry

  • Construction Area / Authorized Personnel Only — controls access and warns the public at the entry to the work area (1926.200, 1926.201 for traffic control)
  • Hard Hat / Eye Protection Required Beyond This Point — post at the entry to any PPE-required area (1926.200(h), 1926.100, 1926.102)

PPE Required (post where the hazard exists)

  • Hard Hat Area (1926.100)
  • Eye Protection Required (1926.102)
  • Hearing Protection Required (1926.101)
  • Respirator / Dust Mask Required (1910.134)

Hazard Warnings

  • Danger: High Voltage / Electrical (1926.200(b), 1926.403)
  • Caution: Fall Hazard / Open Hole / Floor Opening (1926.501)
  • Warning: Silica Dust Hazard (1926.1153, 1910.1200)
  • No Smoking (post near fuels and flammable storage) (1926.151(a)(3))
  • Caution: Overhead Work / Falling Objects (1926.200(c))

Emergency & Information

  • First Aid Kit location (1926.50)
  • Fire Extinguisher location (1926.150)
  • Exit / Emergency Exit (1926.200(g))
  • Emergency Information posting with hospital, address, and phone numbers (1926.50(f)) — this one is required; template below

Emergency Information Posting

OSHA 1926.50(f) requires the telephone numbers of physicians, hospitals, and ambulances to be conspicuously posted where 911 is not the sole means of contact. Fill this in for each site and post it by the entry or job box.

Where to Get Signs

  • Buy pre-printed ANSI Z535 signs from any safety supplier (Grainger, ULINE, MySafetySign, or a local supply house); keep a small set of common PPE and danger signs in the trailer.
  • Print your own: use the correct signal word and color from the table above, large bold type, and a pictogram where possible. Laminate or sleeve them for weather.
  • OSHA 1926.200 — Accident prevention signs and tags
  • OSHA free publications and posters (includes the required Job Safety and Health "It's the Law" poster)
