How I run jobs with Claude

What I hand it, what it can read on its own, and what comes out the other end. Everything on this page is from real jobs in the last two weeks.

Jerry Tyrrell, Project Manager

01

The picture

Claude sits between the systems we already use. I hand it a voice note, it reads what it needs, and it writes back into those same systems.

It can't hear my voice notes or see my photos on its own. I give it those in chat. Voice-to-text on the phone makes that a thirty second job.

02

One voice note, four outputs

This is the part that changed how I work. I talk for a minute after leaving a site and the record keeping happens without me typing any of it.

Just left Dorsey. Kathy wants the wall by the fridge out so the entry is wider, with a finished wood piece next to the fridge. There's an old capped drain in that wall that has to get cut flush and recapped, and the water lines have to move, so plumber time plus finish work. Tell her I'll have the change order to her by end of day tomorrow. Log it, and I still need to get her siding samples.
Claude splits it four ways
Email

Reply to Kathy

Short, in my voice, answers her questions in order, commits to end of day tomorrow. Drafted for me to read.

BuilderTrend

Progress, issues, subs, materials, on our template. Posted, not drafted.

Calendar

"Dorsey: write fridge wall change order, send to Kathy today," with the scope in the description.

Brief

Siding samples

Nothing to order yet, so it lands as an open item in tomorrow's brief instead of the tracker.

Send it.

Rambling is fine. It sorts out what goes where.

B. A daily log from a site visit

Log this on the Tedford job. Met with Max and Sally, went over the change order, they took some items out and added others. Jason the painter walked the exterior with us, he's writing his own proposal direct to them for the siding. Light fixture is a 4 inch base. Claude opens BuilderTrend, picks the right Tedford job, sorts the note onto our template
Posted to Tedford, Sally & Max 260119, dated Tue Sep 29. Progress, Issues, Subs and Materials filled in. Want me to revise change order 0002 with the items they added and removed?
Before, this log would have been three lines typed in the truck, or not written at all. Now it is the full conversation, sorted under the right headings, in my words. It posts without waiting on me because I told it to; that's a setting I chose after a few weeks of reading them first.

C. Revising the change order from that log

Yes, update the Tedford change order. Keep the door locks, tile, address numbers, closet knobs. Exterior painting is trim touch-up only, no siding. Drop the floor joist. Add the 220 circuit for the compressor and the welder plug, the dining room globe, and the smoke detectors.
Claude opens CO 0002 and edits the line items

I price it and I send it. BuilderTrend has no connection for this, so Claude works it through the browser the same way I would, just faster.

04

What actually changed

Not everything, but the parts of the day that used to be typing and searching.

Before

  • Daily logs written when I got to it. Short, or skipped.
  • Opened Outlook constantly. Searched for what a client said three weeks ago.
  • Change orders and proposals written from scratch at a desk.
  • Promises made in email lived in my head until they didn't.
  • Order dates in vendor emails, not in the tracker.
  • Walked into meetings on memory.

Now

  • I talk into my phone leaving the site. The log is posted before I'm back on the road, and it's better than what I used to write.
  • I open email less. When I need something out of it, Claude finds it.
  • Change orders and proposals start from what I said on site. I edit instead of write.
  • If I promise it, it's on the calendar with the detail attached.
  • Orders incoming gets reconciled against email daily.
  • A brief every evening for tomorrow, built from the logs and threads behind each calendar item.

Rough edges

Sometimes I have to explain myself a second time, a job name or a detail it got wrong. It's fast enough by voice that it's worth it.

BuilderTrend has no API, so daily logs and change orders go in through the browser. Slower than email or calendar, but it works.

What I still do myself

Read every client email before it sends. Price the change orders. Make the calls to clients who don't email. Decide what goes in the proposal.

What it remembers

How I write. Who our vendors are and how orders go to them. That Max Tedford only takes phone calls. That the Wednesday 10:00 check-in is a reminder, not a real block. It gets more useful the longer you use it.

05

If you want to try it

Start with one thing. The daily log is the easiest win.

1. Connect Outlook

Email and calendar are where most of the value is, and it's a one-time connection in Claude's settings. Carpenters and runners don't have company email, so this part is for PMs and coordinators.

2. Tell it who you are

Your role, which jobs are yours, how you like to write. Paste in three of your own sent emails and say "write like this."

3. Talk, don't type

Use the microphone on your phone. Ramble. Say the job name. It sorts the rest out.

4. Read it before it goes

Anything to a client, read it first. Internal notes and logs you'll trust pretty quickly.

5. Ask it where you're at

Once a few logs are in, ask "where are we at on the Smith job?" That's when it clicks.

6. Come find me

I'll sit with anyone who wants to set this up. Half an hour is enough to get the first log posted.
