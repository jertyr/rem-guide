Site Set Up

Mobilization is the work of turning a home or business into a jobsite: dumpsters, portable toilets, paperwork, signs, lockboxes, and site protection.

Quick Reference

  • Order the dumpster and mark the drop spot; set 4 skid plates (2x12-8's) under the wheels
  • Order the portable toilet; pumper truck must park within 20-40 feet
  • Print and stage paperwork: permit visible for inspectors, dated plan sets on site
  • Take pre-demo photos of all work areas
  • Plant the jobsite sign (check covenants and city regs first)
  • Set up the lockbox or temporary electronic lock
  • Confirm pet policy with the client and post signs at crew entries
  • Stock the safety bag on day one

Dumpster

  • Roll-off dumpsters are almost always a better value than running debris in pickups or trailers; a 30-yard bin often saves multiple dumping trips and a lot of labor
  • Make sure the bin fits: most 30-yarders are 7'6" wide and 22' long. Check for overhead wires and tree branches, and clear the approach
  • Mark the delivery spot and drop 4 skid plates (2x12-8's) where the wheels will sit to protect the driveway
  • Notify the property owner a week ahead of the drop
  • Most companies charge a flat fee for 2-4 weeks, then rental charges apply. Start with a 30-yard bin and downsize as the job moves along
  • Keep masonry, dirt, and shingles out of general debris bins; they trigger fees and headaches
  • Don't overfill; you'll pay extra and get calls to level the load
  • Client communication:
    • Tell clients when bins are coming or being swapped so cars are out of the way
    • Explain driveway risk: scratching and rust. Always use the skid plates
    • Jurisdictions may prohibit street placement without a permit, which can cost as much as the dumpster itself

Portable Toilets

  • Rent a unit (about $100-200/month) instead of using a bath in the home; it keeps cleaning, stocking, and awkward situations off the client's house
  • Pick a spot close enough to the driveway or street; pumper trucks need to park within 20-40 feet of the unit
  • Mark the chosen spot before delivery
  • You can reposition the unit yourself when empty, but suppliers don't like their toilets sliding around on concrete
  • Keep the toilet out of sight from the street if possible; otherwise strangers may help themselves
  • Tell the client a portable toilet is arriving, and schedule pickup at the end of the job

Paperwork

  • Print and bring: permit, blueprints, window and door specs, cabinet drawings, scope of work or proposal, appliance/plumbing/lighting specs, project schedule, and the neighbor letter
  • Put the permit in the front window or job box for inspectors
  • File everything else where trades can get at it but safe from damage; a job box or site folder works
  • Date your printouts so the most current set is clearly marked; don't let anyone build from outdated plans
  • Make a second "Site Set" of blueprints for inspections; print extras or key pages for trades. Laminating heavily used pages can be worthwhile
  • Take photos of all work areas before demolition begins
  • The neighbor letter introduces you to the neighbors and gives them your contact info; proactive communication heads off conflicts

Jobsite Sign

  • A branded sign helps trades, inspectors, and clients find your site
  • Check neighborhood covenants and city regs before planting it
  • Place it near the road where it's visible
  • Straighten it if it gets knocked crooked; a sloppy sign projects a sloppy builder

Lockbox and Access

  • Get a key and stash it in a coded lockbox near your main entry
  • Attach the box to something sturdy, but not the door handle (avoids scratches)
  • A temporary electronic lock on a new door is even easier; take the lock to your next job
  • Sometimes a garage door code will substitute

Pet Policy

  • Before work starts, confirm whether pets are on site and set expectations with the client
  • Post a sign at every entry point used by the crew and subs
  • Pets can escape through open doors, get into materials or debris, or create liability if they bite a worker

Safety Equipment

  • Bring a safety bag or kit on day one: hard hats, safety glasses, ear protection, dust masks, N95 respirators, first aid kit, fire extinguisher, and high-visibility vests if working near traffic
  • Keep it in the job box or a central location where anyone on site can access it; restock throughout the project
  • See the Safety page for the full citation-backed equipment list
