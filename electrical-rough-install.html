Electrical Rough Install

Electrical rough covers all wiring, boxes, and panel work that happens before insulation and drywall. This is the one chance to get power everywhere it needs to go without tearing into finished surfaces later. The checklist below doubles as a planning tool for homeowners: walk through every room and think about what plugs in, charges, lights up, or needs a switch.

Prerequisites

  • Electrical and low-voltage permits pulled; electrician has filed for their permit after the main building permit is in hand
  • Electrical plan created covering lighting locations, switching plan, special outlet locations, and equipment selections with electrical requirements
  • Lighting and outlet locations laid out and marked on framing
  • Appliance schedule printed with amp draw and voltage for every piece of equipment
  • Framing complete; blocking installed for boxes near doorways
  • Device type and color selected (switches, outlets, plates)

Process

  • Layout
    • Walk the job with the electrician; review the electrical plan, lighting layout, and switching
    • Mark box locations on studs and joists
    • Confirm panel location and capacity
    • Coordinate low-voltage runs: data, camera, speaker, and doorbell locations
  • Panel and Feeders
    • Set panel and run feeder/service wiring
    • Label circuits as they are pulled
  • Branch Circuits
    • Run wiring for all outlets, switches, and dedicated circuits
    • Install boxes at marked locations
    • Dedicated circuits required by code: range/oven (NEC 220.55), dishwasher (NEC 422.10(A)), dryer (NEC 220.54), furnace/boiler (NEC 422.12), water heater (NEC 422.13), bathroom receptacles 20A (NEC 210.11(C)(3)), kitchen small appliance circuits 20A (NEC 210.11(C)(1))
    • Dedicated circuits recommended: refrigerator, microwave, garbage disposal, sump pump, garage/outdoor equipment
  • Lighting Circuits
    • Run wiring for all ceiling, wall, undercabinet, in-cabinet, and exterior lights
    • Install recessed light housings (IC-rated where adjacent to insulation)
    • Wire switches, dimmers, and 3-way/4-way switching per plan
  • Low Voltage
    • Run data/ethernet to planned locations
    • Run coax or conduit for TV prep locations
    • Run wiring for security cameras, doorbell, and alarm if applicable
    • Confirm internet router/equipment location and run home-runs
  • Special Equipment
    • Wire bath fan/light combos
    • Wire range hood
    • Wire heated floors (thermostat location and dedicated circuit)
    • Wire EV charger circuit if applicable
    • Wire generator interlock or transfer switch if applicable
  • Protection and Firestopping
    • Install nail plates on all wires within 1.25" of stud edge (NEC 300.4(A)(1))
    • Fire-caulk and foam all penetrations through fire-rated assemblies
    • Verify fireblocking is intact around all wire penetrations
  • Temporary Power and Lighting
    • Provide temporary lighting and power for the duration of construction
    • Ensure temporary panel is GFCI protected

What Needs Power? Room-by-Room Checklist

Walk this list before rough begins. Once drywall is up, adding circuits is expensive. See the full standalone Electrical Device Checklist for a printable version.

Kitchen

  • Refrigerator
  • Range / cooktop / wall oven
  • Microwave
  • Dishwasher
  • Range hood
  • Garbage disposal
  • Undercabinet lighting
  • In-cabinet lighting
  • Island pop-up outlet
  • Charging drawer / station
  • Coffee maker cabinet outlet
  • Stand mixer outlet
  • Under-sink motion light
  • Instant hot water dispenser
  • Counter receptacles per code

Bathrooms

  • Exhaust fan / light combo
  • Vanity light
  • Heated floor
  • Heated towel bar
  • Toe-kick heater
  • Lighted mirror
  • Bidet toilet seat
  • Electric toothbrush / razor outlet
  • GFCI outlets

Living / Family Room

  • TV prep
  • Soundbar / surround speaker wiring
  • Lamp outlets
  • Fireplace blower / electric fireplace
  • Sconce lights
  • Floor outlet

Bedrooms

  • Nightstand outlets (USB)
  • TV prep
  • Closet light
  • Ceiling fan / light combo
  • Sconce lights

Home Office

  • Desk outlets (power + USB + data)
  • Printer outlet
  • Data drops

Laundry

  • Washer
  • Dryer (electric 240V or gas 120V)
  • Iron / steamer outlet
  • Overhead lighting

Utility / Mechanical

  • Water heater
  • Furnace / boiler
  • Sump pump
  • Radon fan
  • Chest freezer
  • Spare refrigerator
  • Dehumidifier
  • Sump battery backup

Garage

  • Garage door opener(s)
  • EV charger
  • Workbench outlets
  • Chest freezer / spare fridge
  • Overhead lighting
  • Motion lights

Gym / Exercise Room

  • Treadmill / equipment outlet
  • TV prep
  • Sound system

Exterior

  • Exterior outlets (GFCI)
  • Exterior door lights
  • Landscape lighting transformer
  • Security cameras
  • Doorbell
  • Holiday lighting outlet (roofline / soffits)
  • Pool / hot tub
  • Grill area outlet
  • Irrigation controller

Whole-House

  • Smoke detectors (interconnected)
  • CO detectors
  • Internet router / network equipment
  • Whole-house surge protector
  • Generator interlock / transfer switch
  • Doorbell transformer
  • Central vacuum outlets
  • Battery vacuum charger mount
  • Stairway lighting (3-way switches)

Inspections

  • Electrical Rough Inspection: Inspector verifies wire sizing, box fill, grounding, AFCI/GFCI protection, nail plates, and circuit identification
  • Low Voltage Rough Inspection: Separate inspection for data, camera, alarm, and speaker wiring where required by jurisdiction

Best Practices

  • Walk the room-by-room checklist with the homeowner before rough begins — this is the cheapest time to add circuits
  • Pull appliance spec sheets and have them on site for the electrician
  • Label every circuit at the panel during rough, not after
  • Run conduit or low-voltage wiring for future use (EV charger, cameras, speakers) even if not terminating now
  • Photograph all rough-in wiring before insulation goes in
  • Confirm recessed light housings are IC-rated where they will contact insulation; maintain 3" clearance from non-IC-rated fixtures
  • Coordinate with HVAC and plumber to avoid routing conflicts in shared stud bays and joist spaces

Client Communication

  • Walk clients through the room-by-room checklist before rough — once drywall is up, adding circuits is expensive
  • Explain the electrical panel: breakers, sub-panels, and generator interlock if installed
  • Show clients electrical and water shutoffs as part of project turnover
  • Review smoke/CO detector operation: interconnected detectors (one alarms, all alarm), clean with compressed air if nuisance tripping, leave the home immediately if CO alarm sounds
  • Advise that temporary power interruptions will occur during the switch from temporary to permanent service
