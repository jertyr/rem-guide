Interior Trim

Interior trim covers everything from hanging doors to running crown. The typical install order is cabinets, doors, casing, baseboard, and crown. Don't forget the small stuff: glue, shims, nails, shoe molding, cove, closet shelving, and crawl space doors.

Quick Reference

  • Install order: cabinets, doors, casing, baseboard, crown
  • Glue every miter joint and scarf joint
  • Exterior doors: check extension jambs and shoe molding
  • Vacuum out all drawers and cabinets before handoff
  • Adjust all doors and drawers after install
  • Install restrictor clips on all cabinets with tip-over risk
  • Label extra hardware and store securely on site

Prerequisites

  • Drywall finished and primed (Level 4 minimum for painted trim areas)
  • Flooring installed in areas receiving baseboard, unless using baseboard to cover flooring edge
  • Paint first coat on walls complete, or at minimum primer. Trim caulking and touch-up happen after trim is installed
  • HVAC running and house conditioned. Wood trim needs stable humidity to prevent gaps at joints after install
  • Stain-grade trim: have finish samples approved by client before starting. Grain variation between samples and full runs causes disputes
  • Countertops templated or installed (affects backsplash trim and end panels)
  • All blocking and backing in place for cabinets, handrails, and heavy fixtures

Materials and Tools

  • Casing (door and window)
    • Colonial, craftsman, or ranch profiles. Match existing in remodels
  • Baseboard
    • MDF for paint-grade (most common). Poplar or pine for stain-grade
  • Crown molding (if specified)
  • Shoe molding / quarter round
  • Cove molding (inside corners, stair nosings)
  • Extension jambs (for windows and exterior doors where jamb is narrower than wall depth)
  • Door shoe / threshold trim (exterior doors)
  • Closet shelving and rod
    • Wire shelf systems (ClosetMaid, Rubbermaid) or wood shelf with cleat and pole sockets
  • Crawl space access doors
  • Shims (wood and composite)
  • Wood glue (Titebond II or III for miter joints)
  • Fasteners
    • 15-gauge finish nails for casing and baseboard (2" to 2-1/2")
    • 18-gauge brad nails for shoe, cove, and delicate trim (1" to 1-1/2")
    • 16-gauge for crown molding into framing
  • Caulk (paintable latex for gaps between trim and wall)

Process

  • Cabinets
    • Install upper cabinets first, then base cabinets
    • Locate and mark studs. Cabinets must be screwed into framing, not just drywall
    • Level and shim base cabinets starting from the high point of the floor
    • Use cabinet screws through the back rail, not through the face frame, for a clean look
    • Install restrictor clips on tall cabinets and any with tip-over risk
    • Scribe fillers and end panels to walls as needed
  • Interior Doors
    • Hang doors and shim jambs plumb and square
    • Check reveals on all four sides. 1/8" even gap (nickel thickness) is the standard
    • Pre-hung doors: set hinge side first, shim behind every hinge, then set strike side
    • Use pairs of shims (opposing tapers) for a flat bearing surface
    • French doors: install as a unit, shim hinge jambs first, check that both doors meet evenly at astragal
    • Pocket doors: install split jamb, verify door slides freely before casing. Hardware goes in last
    • Install hardware last (hinges, knobs/levers, stops). Route for hinges before hanging if not pre-mortised
  • Casing
    • Set reveal line on jamb edge (typically 3/16" to 1/4"). Mark with a combination square and pencil
    • Including apron and interior sill where applicable
    • Backband over casing if specified
    • Glue every miter joint before nailing
    • Pin miters together with 18-gauge brads or 23-gauge pins to prevent opening
  • Baseboard
    • Inside corners: cope the joint rather than mitering for a tighter long-term fit
    • Outside corners: miter, glue, and pin. Apply glue to the miter faces before closing
    • Scarf joints on long runs. Locate over a stud when possible
    • Nail into studs or bottom plate
  • Crown Molding
    • Install upside down and backwards on the miter saw (spring angle). Mark the saw fence with tape for consistent positioning
    • Nail into top plate and studs. If no framing at ceiling line, add blocking or use adhesive
    • Cope inside corners the same as baseboard
  • Shoe Molding / Quarter Round
    • Install after baseboard and flooring are complete
    • Nail into the baseboard, not the floor. This lets the floor float and expand
  • Exterior Door Trim
    • Check and install extension jambs where wall depth exceeds door jamb
    • Install shoe molding at threshold
    • Case to match interior doors
  • Closets
    • Install shelving and rod
    • Standard rod height: 66" for single rod, 84" and 42" for double rod
    • Standard shelf depth: 12" for a single shelf with rod
  • Crawl Space Doors
    • Install access panels or doors with trim to match surrounding area
  • Stairs
    • Treads and skirt boards
    • Handrail
    • Balustrade (add for miters at turns and transitions)
  • Hardware
    • Bath hardware (towel bars, robe hooks, TP holders, grab bars)
    • Mirrors
    • Medicine cabinets
    • Shower glass
  • Specialties
    • Wainscote
    • Tongue and groove ceiling
    • Boxed beam or post
    • Mitered beam or post
    • Built-ins
  • Final Adjustments
    • Adjust all cabinet doors and drawers for even gaps and smooth operation
    • Adjust interior doors for consistent reveal and smooth latch engagement
    • Vacuum out all drawers and cabinets
    • Label any extra hardware (hinges, pulls, drawer slides) and store securely on site

Best Practices

  • Glue every miter and scarf joint. Nails hold it in place while glue dries; glue is what keeps it tight long term
  • Acclimate trim material in the house for a few days before installing, especially stain-grade
  • Fill nail holes after caulking and before final paint. Use lightweight spackle for painted trim; color-matched filler for stain-grade
  • Run a bead of paintable caulk along the top edge of baseboard and casing where it meets the wall. Don't caulk the bottom of baseboard to the floor
  • Back-cut miters slightly (1-2 degrees) so the visible face closes tight even if the wall isn't perfectly flat
  • For paint-grade MDF: seal all cut ends with primer or glue before install. Raw MDF edges swell when they absorb moisture from caulk or paint

Client Communication

  • Set expectations on caulking. Trim caulking fills the gap between trim and wall; it is normal and not a sign of poor workmanship. Caulk shrinks slightly on the first dry and may need a second pass before paint
  • Stain-grade trim will show more variation than paint-grade. Confirm client has seen and approved actual stain samples on the actual species being used
  • Cabinet adjustments are part of the process. Doors and drawers settle and may need a second adjustment after the house has been lived in for a few weeks
  • Extra hardware: explain where it's stored and why it's being kept (replacement parts, future additions)
