Tile

Covers custom showers, bath and laundry floors, kitchen backsplashes, and decorative locations. Tile work starts at drywall and runs through the finish phase. Selections drive substrate prep, so lock them in early. For wood and LVP flooring, see Flooring.

Quick Reference

  • Confirm tile selections, pattern, and layout before prep begins
  • Tile size, shape, and thickness affect layout, substrate prep, and transition details
  • Shower walls must be plumb, square, and flat before tile prep starts
  • Decoupling membrane on floors (Schluter Ditra) prevents cracking from seasonal framing movement
  • Walk-in shower flood test is a plumbing inspection; coordinate plumber, tilesetter, and inspector
  • Match thinset (modified vs. unmodified) to the membrane manufacturer's requirements
  • Back-butter any tile with a dimension over 15"
  • Seal natural stone before grouting
  • Caulk change-of-plane transitions; do not grout them

Prerequisites

  • Drywall hung, finished, and primed, or left out of the shower area for the tilesetter to prep
  • HVAC operating and conditioning the building
  • Rough plumbing set and inspected: shower valve, drain location, and pan or tub blocking confirmed
  • Shower walls plumb, square, and flat
  • Subfloor clean, swept, fastened tight, and free of drywall mud, screws, or debris
  • Selections locked: tile, pattern, grout color, caulk color, edge profile, niche size and location
  • Tile, setting materials, membranes, and edge profiles on site and checked against the order for color, lot, and quantity

Process

  • Planning
    • Confirm tile selections, pattern, and layout early
    • Plan thresholds, edges, profiles, grout color, and caulk color
    • Plan niches: size, location, and tile for niche interior
    • Confirm shower walls are plumb, square, and flat before tile prep begins
    • Order 10% overage on standard layouts; complex patterns waste more and are billed at a premium
  • Shower Coordination
    • Builder may leave drywall out of shower area for tilesetter to prep, or prep the shower before drywall so sheetrock ties into the waterproofing for a single flat surface
    • Install shower pan and drain; protect the pan during subsequent work
    • Follow manufacturer instructions for waterproofing (Schluter Kerdi, Laticrete Hydro Ban, etc.)
  • Substrate Prep
    • Clean framing/subfloor; confirm flat, level, plumb, and square
    • Floors may need leveling compound. Prime substrate with tile primer first
    • Mechanically fasten wire mesh for wood-floor leveler pours
    • Use spray foam to dam leveler at edges; tape seams so leveler doesn't leak through
    • Install decoupling membrane on floors (e.g., Schluter Ditra) to prevent tile cracking from seasonal framing movement
  • Flood Test
    • Walk-in showers require a flood test. This is scheduled as a plumbing inspection even though the tilesetter does the waterproofing
    • Coordinate timing between plumber, tilesetter, and inspector
    • Tilesetter plugs the drain and fills the pan with 2" of water before inspection
    • After inspection passes, tilesetter continues with wall tile and floor tile
  • Install
    • Install wall tile after shower pan is set and inspected
    • Set tile with appropriate thinset for the substrate and tile type (modified vs. unmodified per membrane manufacturer requirements)
    • Back-butter large-format tiles (any dimension over 15") for full coverage
    • Seal natural stone before grouting. Grout will stain unsealed stone permanently
    • Grout joints; caulk all change-of-plane transitions (wall-to-floor, wall-to-wall corners) with color-matched sanded caulk

Best Practices

  • Plan transition strips between different flooring types early. Height differences between tile, wood, and LVP need the right profile and the subfloor may need to be built up or routed down to match
  • Protect finished tile after install. Use ram board or similar; no bare cardboard (traps moisture) and no thin plastic alone (tears and lets debris through)
  • Dry-lay the first course of a complex pattern before setting anything. Herringbone and basket weave amplify small layout errors across the whole field
  • Keep tile from the same lot together in one area. Mixing lots in a single room shows as a color shift under raking light

Inspections

  • Walk-in shower flood test is inspected by the plumbing inspector even though the tilesetter does the waterproofing. Schedule it with the plumber
  • Pan is plugged and filled with 2" of water before the inspector arrives
  • No wall tile or floor tile goes on until the flood test passes

Client Communication

  • Complex layouts (herringbone, basket weave, diagonal) are non-standard and billed at a premium. Confirm pricing before ordering tile
  • Approve grout and caulk colors before install. Color-matched caulk at change-of-plane transitions is correct practice, not a defect or a shortcut
  • Natural stone needs sealing before grout and periodic resealing afterward. Porcelain and ceramic do not
  • The flood test holds the shower for a day or more. Nothing else happens in that space while the pan is full and waiting on the inspector
