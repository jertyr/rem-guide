Electrical Rough Install

Electrical rough covers all wiring, boxes, and panel work that happens before insulation and drywall. This is the one chance to get power everywhere it needs to go without tearing into finished surfaces later. The checklist below doubles as a planning tool for homeowners: walk through every room and think about what plugs in, charges, lights up, or needs a switch.

Quick Reference

  • Pull electrical and low-voltage permits before rough begins
  • Give the electrician advance notice: 1 month, confirm at 2 weeks, finalize at 1 week
  • Have the electrical plan on site: lighting locations, switching, special outlets, and equipment specs
  • Have appliance specs printed with amp/voltage requirements
  • Establish device type and color early (white, ivory, light almond, etc.)
  • Decide USB outlet locations and dimmer switch locations before rough
  • Ensure temporary lighting and power are provided during construction
  • Protect all wiring with nail plates where wires are within 1.25" of stud edge (IRC E3802.1)
  • Combustible insulation must be kept 3" minimum from recessed lights, fan motors, and other heat-producing devices unless IC-rated (Michigan Residential Code / IRC)

Prerequisites

  • Electrical and low-voltage permits pulled; electrician has filed for their permit after the main building permit is in hand
  • Electrical plan created covering lighting locations, switching plan, special outlet locations, and equipment selections with electrical requirements
  • Lighting and outlet locations laid out and marked on framing
  • Appliance schedule printed with amp draw and voltage for every piece of equipment
  • Framing complete and inspected; blocking installed for boxes near doorways
  • HVAC and plumbing rough complete or coordinated to avoid conflicts
  • Device type and color selected (switches, outlets, plates)
  • Dimmer and USB outlet locations decided

Process

  • Layout
    • Walk the job with the electrician; review the electrical plan, lighting layout, and switching
    • Mark box locations on studs and joists
    • Confirm panel location and capacity
    • Coordinate low-voltage runs: data, camera, speaker, and doorbell locations
  • Panel and Feeders
    • Set panel and run feeder/service wiring
    • Label circuits as they are pulled
  • Branch Circuits
    • Run wiring for all outlets, switches, and dedicated circuits
    • Install boxes at marked locations
    • Dedicated circuits for: range/oven, dishwasher, microwave, fridge, washer, dryer, furnace/boiler, water heater, garbage disposal, bathroom circuits, garage circuits, outdoor circuits
  • Lighting Circuits
    • Run wiring for all ceiling, wall, undercabinet, in-cabinet, and exterior lights
    • Install recessed light housings (IC-rated where adjacent to insulation)
    • Wire switches, dimmers, and 3-way/4-way switching per plan
  • Low Voltage
    • Run data/ethernet to planned locations
    • Run coax or conduit for TV prep locations
    • Run wiring for security cameras, doorbell, and alarm if applicable
    • Confirm internet router/equipment location and run home-runs
  • Special Equipment
    • Wire bath fan/light combos
    • Wire range hood
    • Wire heated floors (thermostat location and dedicated circuit)
    • Wire EV charger circuit if applicable
    • Wire generator interlock or transfer switch if applicable
  • Protection and Firestopping
    • Install nail plates on all wires within 1.25" of stud edge
    • Fire-caulk and foam all penetrations through fire-rated assemblies
    • Verify fireblocking is intact around all wire penetrations

What Needs Power? Room-by-Room Checklist

Use this list during planning to catch anything that needs a circuit, outlet, or dedicated wiring. It is easy to overlook items that are added late or seem minor but require power roughed into the wall before drywall.

Kitchen

  • Refrigerator (dedicated circuit)
  • Range / cooktop / wall oven (240V dedicated circuit)
  • Microwave (dedicated circuit)
  • Dishwasher (dedicated circuit)
  • Range hood / exhaust fan
  • Garbage disposal (switched outlet or hardwired)
  • Undercabinet lighting
  • In-cabinet lighting (interior cabinet lights, glass-front cabinets)
  • Island pop-up outlet or flush-mount outlet
  • Charging drawer or charging station
  • Stand mixer / coffee maker dedicated outlet (in cabinet or at counter)
  • Motion-activated light under sink
  • Instant hot water dispenser
  • Counter receptacles per code (every 4 ft along countertop, GFCI protected)

Bathrooms

  • Fan/light combo (or separate exhaust fan and light)
  • Vanity light fixture
  • Heated floor (dedicated circuit + thermostat)
  • Heated towel bar
  • Toe-kick heater
  • Lighted mirror or mirror with built-in defogging
  • Bidet toilet seat (outlet behind or beside toilet)
  • Rechargeable toothbrush / electric razor outlet
  • GFCI outlets per code

Living / Family Room

  • TV prep (outlet + data/coax behind the TV location)
  • Soundbar or surround-sound speaker wiring
  • Floor lamps / table lamp outlet locations
  • Fireplace (gas valve + power for blower or electric fireplace circuit)
  • Sconce lighting
  • Floor outlet for island furniture (desks, couches away from walls)

Bedrooms

  • Nightstand outlets (both sides of bed, consider USB)
  • TV prep (outlet + data behind TV location)
  • Closet lighting (switched, LED)
  • Ceiling fan / light combo (fan-rated box + proper switching)
  • Sconce lights

Home Office / Study

  • Desk outlet cluster (power + USB + data)
  • Printer / scanner outlet
  • Dedicated circuit if heavy equipment
  • Data drops for hardwired internet

Laundry

  • Washer (120V dedicated circuit)
  • Dryer (240V dedicated circuit, or gas dryer 120V + gas line)
  • Iron / steamer outlet
  • Undercabinet or overhead lighting

Utility / Mechanical Room

  • Water heater (electric: 240V dedicated; gas: 120V for controls)
  • Furnace / boiler
  • Sump pump
  • Radon fan
  • Chest freezer
  • Spare refrigerator
  • Dehumidifier
  • Battery backup / UPS for sump

Garage

  • Garage door opener(s)
  • EV charger (240V, 50A dedicated circuit — plan even if not installing now)
  • Workbench outlets
  • Chest freezer / spare fridge
  • Overhead lighting
  • Exterior motion lights at garage doors

Gym / Exercise Room

  • Treadmill / equipment outlet (20A dedicated if motorized equipment)
  • TV prep (outlet + data)
  • Sound system

Exterior

  • Exterior outlets (GFCI, weatherproof cover — front, back, sides of house)
  • Exterior door lighting
  • Landscape lighting transformer outlet
  • Security cameras (power + data or PoE)
  • Doorbell (wired smart doorbell or traditional)
  • Holiday lighting outlet at roofline or soffits
  • Pool / hot tub circuit (dedicated, GFCI)
  • Exterior grill area outlet
  • Irrigation controller

Whole-House / Miscellaneous

  • Smoke detectors (interconnected — one alarms, all alarm)
  • CO detectors
  • Internet router / network equipment location (power + data home-runs)
  • Whole-house surge protector at panel
  • Generator interlock or transfer switch
  • Doorbell transformer
  • Central vacuum outlet locations
  • Battery vacuum wall-mount charging location
  • Stairway lighting (3-way switching top and bottom)

Inspections

  • Electrical Rough Inspection: Inspector verifies wire sizing, box fill, grounding, AFCI/GFCI protection, nail plates, and circuit identification
  • Low Voltage Rough Inspection: Separate inspection for data, camera, alarm, and speaker wiring where required by jurisdiction
  • Schedule inspections early — electricians are often booked out; get on the inspector's calendar as soon as rough is complete

Best Practices

  • Walk the room-by-room checklist with the homeowner before rough begins — this is the cheapest time to add circuits
  • Pull appliance spec sheets and have them on site for the electrician
  • Label every circuit at the panel during rough, not after
  • Run conduit or low-voltage wiring for future use (EV charger, cameras, speakers) even if not terminating now
  • Photograph all rough-in wiring before insulation goes in
  • Confirm recessed light housings are IC-rated where they will contact insulation; maintain 3" clearance from non-IC-rated fixtures
  • Coordinate with HVAC and plumber to avoid routing conflicts in shared stud bays and joist spaces

Final Install Reminders

  • Have the electrician install appliance outlets first so appliances can go in as soon as they arrive
  • Most light fixtures do not include bulbs — have bulbs on site before final electrical install
  • Confirm device color and type matches selections (switches, outlets, plates)
  • USB outlet locations installed per plan
  • Dimmer switches installed at correct locations
  • Test all GFCI and AFCI breakers/outlets

Client Communication

  • Walk clients through the room-by-room checklist before rough — once drywall is up, adding circuits is expensive
  • Explain the electrical panel: breakers, sub-panels, and generator interlock if installed
  • Show clients electrical and water shutoffs as part of project turnover
  • Review smoke/CO detector operation: interconnected detectors (one alarms, all alarm), clean with compressed air if nuisance tripping, leave the home immediately if CO alarm sounds
  • Advise that temporary power interruptions will occur during the switch from temporary to permanent service
