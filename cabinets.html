Cabinets

Cabinet install sets up everything after it: countertop template, appliance fit, backsplash, and trim. Boxes that are level, straight, and solidly fastened make the rest of the kitchen or bath go together; boxes that are off by 1/8" show up in every trade that follows.

Quick Reference

  • Inspect every box against the order and for damage within a few days of delivery
  • Lay out from the high point of the floor
  • Fasten through the hanging rails into studs or blocking. Never drywall only
  • Join face frames to each other before fastening to the wall
  • Plan fillers and scribes at walls and corners before the first box goes up
  • Verify appliance openings against the spec sheets, not the plan
  • Bases level, secured, and appliance openings confirmed before countertop template

Prerequisites

  • Blocking installed at framing for uppers, hood, tall cabinets, and grab bars. See Framing
  • Plumbing and electrical roughed to the cabinet layout, including under-cabinet lighting, appliance circuits, and disposal
  • Drywall finished and primed. Paint the walls behind the cabinets first if the color is already picked
  • Flooring decision made: whether the finish floor runs under the cabinets or stops at them. Floating floors stop at the cabinets so they are not pinned
  • Appliance spec sheets on site, including sink, faucet, cooktop, hood, and dishwasher
  • Cabinets delivered, inspected, and stored in the conditioned space

Materials and Tools

  • Fasteners
    • #10 cabinet screws, 2 1/2" to 3", washer or pan head, into studs or blocking
    • Shorter cabinet screws or clamps-and-screws for joining face frames
  • Shims, composite preferred since they do not compress or rot
  • Laser or 4' and 6' levels, straightedge
  • Cabinet lift or temporary ledger for uppers
  • Face frame clamps, scribe tools, hole saws
  • Hardware drilling jig
  • Fillers, end panels, toe kick, crown, light rail, dishwasher brackets

Process

  • Layout
    • Find the high point of the floor along every cabinet run
    • Snap a level line for base cabinet height, typically 34 1/2" above the high point
    • Snap the line for the bottom of the uppers, typically 54" off the floor for 18" between countertop and uppers
    • Mark stud and blocking locations above and below both lines
    • Mark plumbing, electrical, and vent penetrations on the wall to transfer into the boxes
  • Uppers
    • Pull doors and shelves, and label them by box
    • Start at a corner and work out
    • Support each box on a lift or a ledger screwed to the line
    • Clamp and screw face frames flush to the next box, then fasten through the top and bottom rails
    • Check plumb on the face and level across the bottoms as you go
  • Bases
    • Start at the corner or the high point
    • Shim to the line, level front to back and side to side
    • Join face frames, then fasten to the wall through the rails
    • Cut plumbing and electrical openings in the backs and bottoms before setting the sink base
    • Scribe fillers and end panels to walls that are not straight
    • Fasten islands to the floor with cleats or blocking so they cannot shift
  • Appliance openings
    • Measure each opening against its spec sheet: width, height, and depth
    • Standard dishwasher opening is 24" wide by 34 1/2" high
    • Confirm refrigerator depth and door swing clear the adjacent cabinets and walls
    • Confirm hood width and mounting height against the cooktop and hood spec sheets
  • Finish
    • Toe kick, end panels, crown, and light rail
    • Rehang doors and drawers; adjust for even reveals
    • Drill hardware with a jig so every pull lines up
    • Protect the tops of bases with cardboard until template

Best Practices

  • Unbox and check everything early. Damage and shortages are fast to fix in week one and slow in week six
  • Plan corner fillers so drawers and doors clear handles and the adjacent run
  • Use structural cabinet screws. Drywall screws snap under load
  • Keep the level line as the reference and shim to it. Do not chase a bad floor box by box
  • Set the sink base and check plumbing fit before the rest of the run locks it in place
  • Leave the countertop fabricator a clean, level, secured run. See Countertops

Inspections

  • Cabinets are not inspected on their own. The electrical final checks countertop receptacles. See Electrical
  • Range hoods over 400 cfm may require makeup air (IRC M1503.6 in recent editions). Confirm with the hood spec and the local code
  • Clearance from the cooktop to combustible cabinets above follows the cooktop and hood installation instructions, commonly 30"

Client Communication

  • Cabinets are usually the long-lead item on a kitchen. The selection has to be final before anything else can be scheduled
  • Stained wood varies in color from door to door; that is the material, not a defect
  • Painted cabinets can show hairline lines at joints as the wood moves
  • Doors and drawers may need a hinge adjustment after the first season
  • Hand off the touch-up kit and the manufacturer's care instructions
