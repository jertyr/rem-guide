Painting

Painting is sequenced after trim is installed, caulked, and filled. The general order is: prep and protect, stain work first, then prime raw wood and trim, spray trim and doors, paint ceilings, cut in and roll walls.

Quick Reference

  • Stain-grade work goes first, before any latex touches the room
  • Prime all raw wood and MDF before topcoating
  • Caulk all paint-grade trim joints before painting
  • Fill nail holes, sand smooth, then spray trim
  • Ceilings before walls. Trim before or after walls depending on crew preference
  • Minimum 4 hours dry time between coats for latex. Check can for recoat windows

Products

  • Walls: Sherwin-Williams Emerald, satin or matte finish
  • Trim and doors: Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel
  • Ceilings: Sherwin-Williams CHB (flat)
  • Stain: per client selection
  • Primer
    • Raw wood / MDF: shellac-based primer (Zinsser BIN) or high-hide latex primer
    • Stain blocking: Zinsser BIN shellac or Kilz Original oil-based for knots and bleed-through
    • New drywall: PVA drywall primer or first coat of finish paint thinned 10%
  • Caulk: paintable latex (DAP Alex Plus or similar)
  • Filler: lightweight spackle for nail holes (DAP Fast 'N Final or similar)

Prerequisites

  • All trim installed, caulked, and nail holes filled
  • Drywall finishing complete (Level 4 minimum for painted surfaces)
  • All dust from sanding and construction cleaned. Dust on surfaces causes adhesion problems and grit in the finish
  • HVAC running. Temperature between 50-85°F for latex products. Low humidity speeds dry time; high humidity causes runs and slow curing
  • Client has approved all paint colors with actual samples on the wall (not just chips). Colors shift significantly between small swatches and full walls, especially grays and greens
  • Stain colors approved on actual wood species being used

Process

  • Prep and Protection
    • Cover floors with drop cloths or rosin paper
    • Cover or mask pre-finished cabinets, countertops, and fixtures
    • Cover furniture if occupied space
    • Remove switch plates, outlet covers, and door hardware. Bag and label per room
    • Fill nail holes with lightweight spackle. Let dry, sand flush with 150-grit
    • Caulk all paint-grade trim to wall joints
    • Scrape any drywall mud drips or texture inconsistencies. Spot-prime repairs
  • Stain-Grade Trim (if applicable)
    • Complete all stain work before starting any latex painting in the same space
    • Sand trim to 150-grit (or per stain manufacturer recommendation)
    • Water-pop the wood: wipe with a damp rag and let dry completely. This raises the grain so the first coat of stain absorbs evenly. Skipping this step causes blotchy results, especially on softwoods
    • Apply stain per client-approved sample
    • Topcoat with clear finish (3 coats)
      • Sand lightly between coats with 220-grit or fine sanding sponge
      • Wipe dust between coats with a tack cloth
      • Water-based poly (Minwax Polycrylic, General Finishes High Performance) for interior. Dries faster and won't amber over time
      • Oil-based poly for high-wear surfaces (treads, handrails) if client accepts the amber tone
  • Prime
    • Prime all raw wood, MDF, and patched areas
    • Spot-prime knots and resinous areas with shellac-based primer (Zinsser BIN) to prevent bleed-through
    • Prime new drywall if not already done during drywall phase
    • Let primer dry fully before sanding. Sand primed trim lightly with 220-grit for a smooth base
  • Trim and Doors
    • Stand doors on sawhorses or door racks in the middle of the room for spraying
    • Spray 2+ coats of trim enamel (Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel)
    • Sand lightly between coats with 220-grit if any roughness or dust nibs
    • Spray gives the smoothest finish on trim and doors. Back-brush or back-roll if spraying in place to prevent runs
    • Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel: recoat after 4 hours minimum. Cures hard like oil-based but cleans up with water
  • Ceilings
    • Paint ceilings before walls
    • Cut in ceiling edges first, then roll. Use a flat finish (CHB) to hide imperfections
    • Roll in one direction, maintaining a wet edge. Start near the window and work away from natural light to spot misses
    • One coat is usually sufficient with CHB on primed drywall. Two coats if covering a dark color or patched ceiling
  • Walls
    • Cut in edges, corners, and around trim
    • Roll walls with finish paint (Emerald satin or matte)
    • Two coats minimum. Cut in and roll the same wall in the same session to avoid lap marks where the cut-in dries before rolling
    • Roll in a "W" pattern, then even out with straight strokes. Maintain a wet edge
    • Use a 3/8" nap roller for smooth walls, 1/2" for light texture. Avoid thick nap on smooth drywall as it leaves stipple
  • Touch-Up and Punch
    • Walk each room in good light. Check for holidays (missed spots), drips, and uneven coverage
    • Touch up with a small brush and the original batch paint. Touch-ups from a different batch or sheen will flash
    • Reinstall switch plates, outlet covers, and hardware
    • Label remaining paint cans with room name and color code. Leave on site for client

Best Practices

  • Box paint: pour all cans of the same color into one container and mix before starting a room. Slight batch variation between cans can show on large walls
  • Keep the room ventilated but avoid fans blowing directly on wet surfaces. Moving air causes uneven drying and dust adhesion
  • Don't thin Emerald products. They are formulated to spray and roll at full viscosity. Thinning reduces coverage and durability
  • Mask off floor transitions and pre-finished surfaces with painter's tape. Remove tape while the last coat is still tacky; pulling after full cure lifts edges
  • Keep a wet edge at all times when rolling. If the leading edge dries before you get back to it, you'll see a lap mark
  • For occupied remodels: plastic off the work area floor to ceiling to contain overspray and dust. Run a box fan with a furnace filter as a temporary air scrubber

Client Communication

  • Colors look different under job lighting vs. natural light vs. evening light. Encourage clients to view samples at multiple times of day before committing
  • Paint sheen matters: matte hides imperfections but marks more easily; satin is more washable but shows wall flaws. Set this expectation early
  • Trim enamel takes 30 days to fully cure. Doors and trim will be dry to the touch quickly but remain soft. Advise against stacking items against trim or slamming doors during the cure period
  • Leave labeled cans on site for touch-ups. Note that touch-ups work best within the first few months; after the wall ages and fades slightly, spot touch-ups may be visible
